## Overview

Turning a vague brief ("make it pop," "something modern") into a concrete design direction is a core
professional skill — and now, a human-AI collaboration skill. This skill structures the journey from
words to design: interrogating the brief, generating distinct creative directions, specifying them
precisely enough to execute (by human or AI tools), and planning production.

## When to use

- Starting a design project from a text brief or rough idea

- Generating multiple creative directions for a client or team

- Briefing designers or AI image tools with precision

- Turning stakeholder ramblings into actionable design specs

- Exploring concepts quickly before committing to production

## Core concepts

- - **The brief is always incomplete.** Your first job is interrogation: audience, goal,
  constraints, references, anti-references ("definitely not like X"). A 15-minute brief
  interrogation saves days of wrong-direction work.
- - **Directions, not variations.** Generate 3 genuinely different creative directions (e.g.,
  "brutalist typographic," "warm editorial illustration," "clean Swiss minimal") — not 3 shades of
  the same idea. Clients choose between philosophies, not decorations.
- - **Specify like a director.** Every direction needs: mood (3 adjectives), visual references,
  color palette (actual values), typography (specific faces or categories), composition principles,
  and what to avoid. Vague direction produces vague design.
- - **Words → moodboard → spec.** Translate the brief into a visual moodboard first (images
  communicate faster than adjectives), then into a written spec. The moodboard aligns feeling; the
  spec aligns execution.
- - **Constraints breed creativity.** Budget, timeline, brand rules, technical limits — treat
  constraints as design inputs, not obstacles. The best directions often come from the tightest
  boxes.
- - **AI as sketch partner.** Use generative tools to visualize directions quickly, but art-direct
  the output: the AI explores, you decide. Never present raw AI output as the final direction
  without curation.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. **Interrogate the brief.** Extract or ask: goal (what should this achieve?), audience (who
   exactly?), message (one sentence), context (where will it live?), constraints (brand, budget,
   timeline), references and anti-references.
2. 2. **Write the creative brief.** One page: objective, audience insight, key message, tone (3
   adjectives), mandatories, deliverables, timeline. Get sign-off — this is the contract against
   scope creep.
3. 3. **Generate 3 directions.** For each: name it, describe the visual language (color, type,
   imagery, composition), show 3-5 reference images, note what makes it distinct and what risks it
   carries.
4. 4. **Specify the chosen direction.** Full spec: palette with values, typefaces with roles,
   imagery style with examples, layout principles, motion/feel notes if relevant, and explicit
   don'ts.
5. 5. **Prototype quickly.** Rough mockups or AI-generated concept visuals of the direction applied
   to the real deliverable. Judge: does it communicate the message? Does it feel right for the
   audience?
6. 6. **Plan production.** Break the deliverable into assets, assign tools and owners, set
   milestones, define review points. The spec becomes the production checklist.
7. 7. **Review against the brief.** Before final delivery: does it achieve the stated goal for the
   stated audience within constraints? The brief is the grading rubric.

## Common pitfalls

- - **Designing from the first adjective.** "Modern" means 50 different things. Interrogate every
  adjective until it's visual: "modern like what? Show me."
- - **Presenting one direction.** One option isn't a choice — it's a demand. Three distinct
  directions invite real feedback and better decisions.
- - **Moodboard as final.** References that inspire but never get translated into specs. The
  moodboard aligns taste; only the spec enables execution.
- - **Ignoring constraints until production.** Discovering the brand font license, the print budget,
  or the CMS limitations after designing. Constraints first, always.
- - **AI output as deliverable.** Uncurated generative output presented as finished design —
  inconsistent, off-brief, often unusable at production quality. AI accelerates exploration;
  judgment finishes the job.
- - **No written brief.** Keeping it all in conversation. When stakeholders disagree later (they
  will), the written brief is the referee.
