## Overview

Template libraries accelerate users: pre-built starting points (documents, workflows, campaigns, designs) that turn blank slates into momentum. This skill covers designing template systems — template anatomy, gallery UX, customization, versioning, and measuring adoption. General SaaS/product guidance, platform-neutral.


Template libraries give users pre-built starting points: workflows, campaigns, documents, and configurations they can customize rather than create from scratch.
Good libraries accelerate adoption and encode best practices; bad ones collect dust.
This skill covers template strategy, design, organization, and the analytics that keep libraries useful.

## Core concepts

**Template anatomy.** Great templates have: a clear purpose (one job), sensible defaults (80% right out of the box), guided customization (what to change, marked clearly), realistic sample content (not lorem ipsum — show the pattern), and documentation (when to use, how to adapt).

**Gallery UX.** Browse by use case (not by internal taxonomy), search that works, preview before committing (screenshots, live demos), difficulty/time estimates ("5-minute setup"), and social proof (popular, ratings, usage counts). The gallery is a store — merchandise it.

**Customization balance.** Templates must be easy to adapt: clearly marked editable zones, variables/tokens for personalization, and guardrails (what shouldn't change). Too rigid = useless; too open = overwhelming. Progressive customization: simple tweaks upfront, deep edits available.

**Use-case organization.** Templates organized by job-to-be-done ("onboard new hires", "run a sprint retro"), industry, and team size. Users think in problems, not features — mirror their mental model.

**Versioning and maintenance.** Templates rot as products change: version them, review quarterly, deprecate outdated ones visibly, and track which templates reference changed features. A gallery of broken templates is worse than no gallery.

**Contribution.** Community/user-submitted templates scale libraries: submission guidelines, quality review, attribution, and curation (featured vs. community tiers). The best template libraries become ecosystems.


**Template anatomy.** Purpose statement (what this solves) → pre-filled content (realistic examples, not lorem ipsum) → customization guidance (what to change) → usage instructions → related templates.
Templates with placeholder content get abandoned — realistic examples teach while they accelerate.
**Organization and discovery.** Categories, tags, search, featured collections, and use-case browsing.
Discovery is the product — the best template undiscovered helps nobody.
Track search queries; failed searches reveal missing templates.
**Quality bars.** Every template: tested end-to-end, reviewed by domain experts, documented, and versioned.
Low-quality templates erode trust in the whole library — curate ruthlessly.
Retire outdated templates; stale templates teach wrong practices.
## Practical workflow

1. **Identify template opportunities.** Where do users start from blank slates? What do power users build repeatedly? What do support/sales recreate per customer? Prioritize by frequency × pain.
2. **Design templates.** Per template: purpose statement, default content (realistic, best-practice), customization points marked, and usage guidance. Test with 3–5 target users — can they adapt it in under 10 minutes?
3. **Build the gallery.** Categories by use case, search, previews, metadata (time to customize, popularity, updated date), and featured curation. Mobile-responsive — people browse everywhere.
4. **Launch and promote.** Announce in-app (empty states link to relevant templates), onboarding (templates as starting points), and content marketing (template roundups drive SEO and signups).
5. **Enable contributions.** Submission process, quality bar, review workflow, and attribution. Seed with official templates; grow with community.
6. **Measure and maintain.** Template views, use rate (started), completion rate (customized and used), time-to-value for template users vs. blank-slate, and ratings. Quarterly: update stale templates, prune unused ones.

**Template quality checklist:** clear purpose → realistic sample content → customization points marked → under 10 min to adapt → usage guidance included → reviewed this quarter → works with current product version.


**Library building process:** identify top 10 user jobs → design templates per job → user-test (can they customize successfully?) → publish with documentation → promote in onboarding → measure usage → iterate.
Start with 10 excellent templates, not 100 mediocre ones.
**Analytics:** views → previews → uses → customization success → outcome correlation.
Track which templates drive activation and retention — invest in winners, fix or retire losers.
## Common pitfalls

- **Lorem ipsum templates.** Placeholder content teaches nothing. Realistic, best-practice examples.
- **Gallery as dumping ground.** Hundreds of uncurated templates. Curate ruthlessly; feature the best.
- **No maintenance.** Templates referencing removed features. Quarterly reviews; visible deprecation.
- **Internal taxonomy.** Organized by product modules instead of user jobs. Mirror user mental models.
- **Over-complex templates.** "Ultimate" templates with 50 sections. Start simple; offer advanced variants.
- **No preview.** Forcing commitment before seeing. Preview everything — screenshots minimum, interactive best.
- **Ignoring adoption data.** Building templates nobody uses. Measure; double down on winners, kill losers.
- **Template sprawl.** Hundreds of overlapping templates. Audit quarterly; merge duplicates, retire unused.
- **No maintenance.** Templates rotting as products change. Assign owners; review on product release cycles.
- **Generic examples.** Lorem ipsum and "Company X" placeholders. Realistic examples teach; placeholders confuse.
- **No version control.** Templates edited without tracking. Version everything — users need to know what changed and when.
- **Over-templating.** Templates for everything stifle judgment. Template the routine; leave room for expertise.
