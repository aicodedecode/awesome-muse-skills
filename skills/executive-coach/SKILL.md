## Overview

At senior levels, success stops being about functional excellence and starts
being about judgment,
influence, and organizational leverage. Executive coaching addresses the
distinct challenges of
directors, VPs, and C-suite leaders: thinking in systems, managing complex
stakeholders,
communicating with boards, and leading through other leaders. The work is
reflective,
confidential-feeling, and ruthlessly practical.

## When to use

- Transitioning to director/VP/C-level scope

- Navigating board, investor, or C-suite stakeholder dynamics

- Developing strategic thinking and organizational influence

- Handling high-stakes decisions with incomplete information

- Working through isolation, imposter feelings, or burnout at senior levels

## Core concepts

- - - **From doing to deciding.** Senior leaders create value through the
  quality of a few
  high-leverage decisions, not the volume of their output. Protect thinking time
like revenue
  depends on it — it does.
- - - **Stakeholder mapping.** Every initiative has supporters, blockers, and
  the indifferent. Map
  them explicitly: who needs to be informed, consulted, won over, or
neutralized? Influence
  campaigns beat org-chart authority.
- - - **Narrative is a leadership tool.** Strategies live or die on the story:
  where are we, where are
  we going, why will we win, what's each person's part? If you can't tell it in
3 minutes, you don't
  have one.
- - - **Leading leaders.** Managing managers requires a gear shift: you set
  context and constraints,
  they own the how. Resist the urge to skip levels or re-litigate their
decisions publicly.
- - - **The loneliness is structural.** Fewer peers, higher stakes, can't vent
  downward. Build an
  external support system: coach, peer group, mentors. This isn't indulgence;
it's maintenance.
- - - **Presence under pressure.** Composure, clarity, and decisiveness in
  crises are what senior
  leaders are actually paid for. Preparation (scenarios, pre-mortems) is what
makes composure
  possible.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. 1. **Establish the mandate.** What's the 6-12 month outcome? (A successful
   org change, a
   board-ready strategy, a leadership brand shift.) Everything orients to this.
2. 2. 2. **360-style diagnosis.** Gather perspectives: what do stakeholders,
   peers, and reports
   experience? Where's the gap between intent and impact? Use real feedback, not
self-assessment
   alone.
3. 3. 3. **Identify the 1-2 shifts.** Senior development isn't a laundry list —
   it's one or two
   behavioral shifts with outsized payoff (e.g., "from operator to owner of the
narrative," "from
   consensus-seeker to decider").
4. 4. 4. **Work live situations.** Coach around real upcoming moments: the board
   presentation, the
   reorg announcement, the difficult peer negotiation. Prepare, rehearse,
debrief.
5. 5. 5. **Build stakeholder strategy.** For key initiatives: map the players,
   their interests, and
   your approach to each. Rehearse the hard conversations.
6. 6. 6. **Install reflection rituals.** Weekly 30-minute review: what decisions
   did I make, what did I
   avoid, where did I add leverage versus activity? Monthly: stakeholder
temperature check.
7. 7. 7. **Measure and adjust.** Quarterly: what's changed in stakeholder
   perception, decision quality,
   and organizational outcomes? Coaching without evidence is just conversation.

## Common pitfalls

- - - **Staying in operator mode.** The most common senior failure: filling the
  calendar with
  execution because strategy feels unproductive. If you're the busiest person in
the room, you're
  not leading it.
- - - **Avoiding necessary conflict.** At senior levels, unresolved peer
  tensions become
  organizational drag. Address them directly and early.
- - - **Communicating strategy once.** Leaders drastically under-communicate.
  The narrative needs
  repeating 7+ times across channels before it lands — then it needs repeating
again.
- - - **No honest feedback channel.** Power distorts what people tell you.
  Actively cultivate 2-3
  truth-tellers with explicit permission to challenge you.
- - - **Burnout disguised as commitment.** Senior roles expand to fill all
  available life. Sustainable
  pace is a strategic choice — exhausted leaders make worse decisions and model
worse norms.
- - - **Confusing activity with influence.** Shipping many initiatives that
  nobody adopts is motion,
  not progress. Senior impact is measured in organizational outcomes, not
personal output.
