# Best Movie Seats

The goal is the best available seats for the whole party,
together, in the specific room the movie is actually playing in.

## Procedure

1. **Identify the exact room, not just the theater:** chain,
   location, auditorium number or name, and format (standard,
   IMAX, Dolby Cinema, other premium large format, 4DX, ScreenX,
   recliner vs stadium seating). The same multiplex has good and
   bad rooms, and the format changes the sweet spot.
2. **Check room-specific sources first:** search the web for the
   theater name plus "best seats" and the auditorium number,
   enthusiast communities for the format (IMAX communities for
   IMAX rooms), and local guides that rate specific auditoriums.
   If a source names rows for this room, those outrank the
   general rules below.
3. **Apply the general rules** to what the seat map offers, in
   this priority order: center block first, then the format's
   sweet-spot rows, then the whole party together.
4. **Seat the party together** in the best available contiguous
   block. Never split the party across rows without the user's
   say-so. If the sweet-spot block cannot hold the party, take
   the best contiguous block nearest the sweet spot and say so.
5. **Report what was checked:** the sources consulted for this
   room (or "no room-specific source found, general rules
   applied"), the seats chosen, and how they sit relative to the
   sweet spot.

Buying itself follows the user's normal ticketing flow and needs
their explicit go-ahead at checkout; this skill only decides
which seats to pick.

## General rules (the consensus)

- **Center block, always.** Dead center of the row, within a few
  seats of the centerline. The picture is symmetrical there and
  the left and right audio balances. Everything else is
  secondary.
- **Two-thirds back is the default row zone.** Roughly two-thirds
  of the way from screen to back wall is where the sound mix is
  calibrated and the sightline matches the standard viewing
  angle. In row terms: rows 13 to 15 of 20, rows 10 to 12 of 15.
  The acceptable band is halfway to two-thirds back.
- **Distance scales with screen width:** sit roughly 1.5 to 2
  times the screen's width away. Bigger screen, further back;
  small screen, the sweet spot moves a few rows forward.
- **Avoid:** the front two or three rows (neck strain, image
  spills past peripheral vision), the back corners (worst sound
  and off-axis picture in the house), extreme side seats, and the
  very back row in big-format rooms (the screen shrinks out of
  the premium field of view).
- **Aisle seats inside the sweet-spot rows are a fair trade** when
  the party values easy exits (kids, long films).
- **Recliner rooms bend the front-row rule a little.** Recliners
  tilt the head back, so rows a bit closer than the standard
  sweet spot are comfortable. Aim for the center of the middle
  riser section.
- **Cross-aisle rows** get foot traffic and latecomer light.
  Prefer the row in front of the walkway, all else equal.
- **3D:** sit at the closer end of the sweet zone, center. The
  glasses cut brightness and depth reads best on the center axis.
- **Seat-map literacy:** confirm which end of the map is the
  screen before counting rows. "Third row" vs "third row from
  the back" is the classic mistake.

## Format rules

- **IMAX giant rooms (1.43:1):** sit further back than standard,
  center, roughly four or five rows from the back. Never the
  front three rows; the screen towers past your field of view.
- **IMAX standard and laser retrofit rooms (1.90:1):** center,
  upper-middle rows, roughly rows 6 to 11 depending on room size.
- **Dolby Cinema:** center, around rows D to F in most rooms
  (larger rooms E to H). The Atmos array aims at this zone and
  the picture is calibrated for this distance. Avoid the first
  two rows and the very back row.
- **4DX:** middle rows, center; the front rows are the splash
  zone. **ScreenX:** center of the standard seating area; the
  side-wall panels distort from the wings.
- **Standard recliner auditoriums:** center block, middle riser
  section, roughly halfway to two-thirds back.
