# Goal Setter

## Overview

Goals fail when vague ('get fit'), disconnected from weekly action, or set and forgotten by February.

Three layers make goals work: outcome (what), measure (how you'll know), process (what you'll do weekly).

SMART and OKRs handle outcome + measure; habits, calendars, and reviews handle process.

Most people over-invest in writing goals beautifully and under-invest in the review cadence that sustains them.

The review cadence matters more than the framework. A goal without reviews is a wish.

## When to use

- Annual or quarterly planning, personal or team
- Turning vague ambitions into trackable, actionable goals
- Setting OKRs for a team or company
- Diagnosing why past goals fizzled out
- Aligning a team's work to a shared direction

## Core concepts

- **SMART goals.**
  Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Best for individual goals with finish lines. 'Achievable' is load-bearing.
- **OKRs.**
  Objectives (qualitative, inspiring) + Key Results (quantitative, 3-5 each). Key results are measurable outcomes, not task lists.
- **Outcome vs. process goals.**
  Outcome: 'lose 10kg.' Process: 'walk 8k steps daily.' You control process; outcomes follow with lag. Manage process daily.
- **Fewer, bigger.**
  3-5 goals per quarter max. Every additional goal dilutes the others. If everything is priority, nothing is.
- **Leading vs. lagging indicators.**
  Lagging (revenue, weight) visible too late to act. Leading (calls made, workouts done) actionable now. Track leading weekly.
- **The review is the system.**
  Weekly check-ins, monthly deeper reviews, quarterly retrospectives. Non-negotiable infrastructure, not optional.
- **Written and visible.**
  One visible dashboard — wall, note, page. Out of sight is out of mind within two weeks.
- **70% confidence target.**
  Ambitious but believable. ~0% perceived chance teaches helplessness; 100% is just a task list.

## Practical workflow

1. **Reflect before setting.**
   Review last period: what worked, didn't, what you learned. Goals without reflection repeat old mistakes with new enthusiasm.
2. **Choose 3-5 goals.**
   Each an outcome with a deadline. Test: exciting enough for hard days, specific enough to measure without argument?
3. **Define measures.**
   2-4 measurable indicators per goal, at least one leading indicator you can influence this week.
4. **Break into quarterly milestones.**
   Annual goals die in Q1 without checkpoints. What must be true at each quarter-end to stay on track?
5. **Translate to weekly actions.**
   1-3 recurring weekly behaviors per goal, on the calendar or task system. Goals live or die in the weekly schedule.
6. **Set the review cadence.**
   Weekly 15-min: score red/yellow/green, one action per yellow/red. Monthly deeper. Quarterly full retro and reset.
7. **Make them visible and shared.**
   One-page dashboard, reviewed weekly. Teams: open OKRs, regular review forum, anyone can see anyone's progress.
8. **Kill dead goals proudly.**
   Quarterly: retire goals that became irrelevant. A dashboard of dead goals generates guilt, not progress.

## Common pitfalls

- **Vague goals.**
  'Improve marketing' isn't a goal. 'Grow newsletter to 5,000 subscribers by Q3' is. Disagreement about achievement = vague.
- **Task-list key results.**
  'Launch campaign' is a task. 'Achieve 20% open rate' is a key result. Tasks belong in the plan, not the goal.
- **Set-and-forget.**
  January goals rediscovered in December. The weekly review is the entire game — without it, skip goal-setting.
- **Too many goals.**
  Twelve goals = zero focus + twelve guilt sources. Kill or defer until 3-5 remain.
- **All outcome, no process.**
  Staring at the scale doesn't lose weight. Define weekly behaviors and track those; outcomes are lagging applause.
- **Unrealistic stretch.**
  OKRs can stretch, but 0%-chance goals teach helplessness. Aim for 60-70% confidence.
- **Conflicting goals.**
  'Ship fast' + 'zero defects' as simultaneous top goals paralyze. Name the trade-off, pick the priority.
- **No sunset.**
  Irrelevant goals lingering on the dashboard. Quarterly retros should kill dead goals without ceremony.
