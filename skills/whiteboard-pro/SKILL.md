# Whiteboard Pro

## Overview

Digital whiteboards (Miro, FigJam, Mural, and equivalents) are infinite canvases for visual collaboration: sticky notes, diagrams, flows, and freeform sketching.

They shine for workshops, retros, journey mapping, brainstorming, and planning — anywhere spatial thinking beats documents.

Pro skill: designing boards people can actually use (structure + guidance), facilitating live sessions, and converting output to action.

## When to use

- Running remote workshops, retros, or brainstorms
- Facilitating design sprints or planning sessions
- Visual collaboration across time zones (async boards)
- Journey mapping, diagramming, or systems thinking
- Teaching or training with visual interaction

## Core concepts

- **Board architecture.**
  Zones with clear labels, flow arrows showing sequence, color coding by category. A board should be navigable without a guide.
- **Templates.**
  Retro, brainstorm, journey map, kanban, 2x2 — start from templates, customize lightly. Templates teach the method implicitly.
- **Facilitation frames.**
  Each activity gets: instructions, example, timer, and a defined output. Frames make async participation possible too.
- **Sticky note discipline.**
  One idea per note, short text, color by category/author. 50-word stickies are mini-docs, not brainstorming.
- **Dot voting.**
  Converge with limited votes per person. Forces prioritization; surfaces real preferences vs. loudest voices.
- **Async boards.**
  Boards that work without live facilitation: clear instructions, examples, deadlines. Essential across time zones.
- **Timers and timeboxes.**
  Visible timers per activity. Whiteboard sessions expand without timeboxes — energy and focus are finite.
- **Export and action.**
  Boards produce decisions and tasks. Export summaries, assign owners, move tasks to trackers. The board is not the deliverable.

## Practical workflow

1. **Define the outcome.**
   Before building: what decision, list, or plan must this board produce? Design backward from the output.
2. **Build the board.**
   Zones in sequence, facilitation frames per activity (instructions + example + timer), color legend, clear start point.
3. **Prepare participants.**
   Share board link + 2-min intro video or instructions ahead. First-time users need onboarding, not just a link.
4. **Facilitate actively.**
   Welcome, explain, demo one activity, timebox strictly, energize between segments, converge with voting.
5. **Manage the loud/quiet.**
   Silent brainstorming first (everyone writes), then share. Dot voting over verbal consensus. Structure protects quiet voices.
6. **Converge deliberately.**
   Cluster, vote, prioritize, decide. End every session with decisions + owners + next steps, captured on the board.
7. **Follow up async.**
   Share board summary + decisions within 24h. Open items get owners and deadlines in the task tracker.
8. **Template the winners.**
   Save successful board designs as team templates. Build a library: retro, planning, brainstorm, review.

## Common pitfalls

- **Blank canvas chaos.**
  'Here's a board, go!' produces mess and silence. Structure + instructions + examples, always.
- **No timeboxes.**
  Open-ended whiteboarding drains energy and overruns. Timers visible, segments tight, breaks scheduled.
- **Facilitation-free.**
  Assuming the board runs itself. Someone must facilitate: guide, timekeep, converge, decide.
- **Sticky novels.**
  Paragraphs on stickies kill scanning and clustering. One idea, few words, always.
- **Skipping convergence.**
  Divergent brainstorms with no voting or prioritizing. The session must end with decisions, not just ideas.
- **Board as archive.**
  Important outcomes living only on the board. Export decisions/tasks to durable systems; boards are working space.
- **Ignoring async participants.**
  Designing only for live attendees. Frames with instructions let timezone-shifted teammates contribute meaningfully.
- **Tool wars.**
  Debating platforms instead of facilitating. Pick one team standard; the facilitation matters 10x more than the tool.
