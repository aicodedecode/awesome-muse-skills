## Overview

This skill covers running social media as a business function: choosing the right platforms, building a sustainable content system, engaging the community, and measuring what matters. It treats social as a channel with business goals (awareness, engagement, traffic, leads, sales), not a vanity exercise.

Vendor-neutral: the frameworks here apply whether you use a scheduling tool, a spreadsheet, or post manually.

## When to use

- Launching or rebooting a brand's social presence
- Building a monthly content calendar
- Deciding which platforms deserve effort (and which to drop)
- Improving engagement rates that have flatlined
- Reporting social performance to stakeholders
- Handling negative comments or a brewing PR issue

- Training new hires or freelancers on the brand's social voice and workflows
- Planning a product launch across social channels
- Deciding whether to invest in paid social vs. organic
- Managing social during product launches
- Handling social media crises
- Building employee advocacy programs
## Core concepts

**Platform-audience fit.** Each platform has a native content language. Short-form video dominates TikTok/Reels/Shorts; professional insight works on LinkedIn; visual storytelling on Instagram; real-time conversation on X. Be excellent on 1–2 platforms rather than mediocre on six.

**Content pillars.** 3–5 recurring themes that map to business goals (e.g., education, social proof, behind-the-scenes, product, community). Pillars make ideation repeatable: every post should fit a pillar.

**The 4:1 value ratio.** Roughly four pieces of useful/entertaining content for every one promotional post. Audiences tolerate promotion when the account consistently gives value.

**Engagement as distribution.** Comments, shares, saves, and DMs are the metrics that drive algorithmic reach. Design posts to earn them: ask questions, post polarizing (but on-brand) takes, create save-worthy resources.

**Community management.** Respond to comments within hours, not days. Have a triage system: praise (thank + amplify), questions (answer helpfully), complaints (acknowledge publicly, resolve privately), trolls (hide/delete per policy, never feed).


**Algorithmic incentives.** Every platform rewards what keeps users on-platform: watch time and completion (video), meaningful comments (not just likes), saves and shares (strongest intent signals), and consistent posting from accounts with engaged followers. Design content for these behaviors explicitly — e.g., loopable video endings boost completion rates.

**Content formats ladder.** Anchor formats (weekly series audiences expect) → experimental formats (tests, 20% of output) → tentpole moments (launches, events). The ladder balances consistency with discovery of new winners.

**Crisis management.** Monitor (social listening alerts) → assess (real crisis or loud minority?) → respond (acknowledge quickly, facts over spin) → resolve → learn.
The 1-hour rule: acknowledge within an hour, even if the full response takes longer.
Never delete criticism (screenshots live forever) — address it.
**Employee advocacy.** Employees' networks dwarf brand followers; authentic employee voices outperform brand accounts.
Provide: content libraries, suggested copy (editable), training, and recognition.
Make sharing easy and voluntary — mandated advocacy reads as inauthentic.
**Social listening.** Track: brand mentions, competitor moves, industry trends, and crisis signals.
Listening informs content, product, and strategy — not just community management.
Weekly listening reports to stakeholders; real-time alerts for spikes.
## Practical workflow

1. **Audit and set goals.** Review current profiles: bio clarity, link in bio, visual consistency, posting frequency, engagement rate. Set 2–3 goals tied to business outcomes (e.g., "drive 500 qualified site visits/month from LinkedIn").
2. **Define audience and pillars.** Write a one-paragraph audience profile per platform. Choose 3–5 content pillars and assign each a weekly quota.
3. **Build the calendar.** Plan 2–4 weeks ahead. For each slot: date, platform, pillar, format, hook/copy draft, visual asset needed, CTA, link. Leave 20% of slots open for reactive/trending content.
4. **Create in batches.** Shoot or design a week's content in one session. Write hooks first — the first line or first 2 seconds decides everything. Keep brand voice consistent; adapt format to platform.
5. **Publish and engage.** Post at times your analytics show your audience is active. Spend 15–30 minutes after posting replying to early comments — early engagement boosts reach. Also engage with others' content daily (prospects, partners, community members).
6. **Measure weekly.** Track per platform: follower growth, engagement rate (engagements ÷ reach), link clicks, and goal conversions. Identify top 10% posts and reverse-engineer why they worked.
7. **Report monthly.** One page: goals vs. actuals, best/worst content with hypotheses, audience insights, next month's experiments. Tie results to business outcomes, not just likes.

**Crisis basics:** pause scheduled posts immediately, acknowledge the issue publicly within hours, move resolution to private channels, post a follow-up when resolved. Never delete legitimate criticism.


**Weekly content retro (30 min):** pull top 3 and bottom 3 posts by engagement rate; for each, write one hypothesis about why. Promote patterns from winners into next week's calendar; kill patterns from losers. This loop is the entire growth engine.

**Content calendar process:** monthly themes → weekly planning (formats, topics, owners) → daily execution → real-time opportunities (newsjacking) → weekly performance review.
Balance planned (70%) with reactive (30%) — all-planned feels robotic, all-reactive feels chaotic.
**Reporting dashboard:** growth (followers, reach) → engagement (rate, saves, shares) → conversion (traffic, signups) → sentiment → competitive benchmarks.
Report monthly to stakeholders; review weekly internally.
Tie metrics to business outcomes wherever possible.
## Common pitfalls

- **Being everywhere.** Spreading thin across platforms guarantees mediocrity. Pick where your audience actually is.
- **Posting without engaging.** Social media is two-way. Accounts that broadcast but never reply stall.
- **Vanity metrics.** Follower counts without engagement are hollow. Optimize for saves, shares, comments, and clicks.
- **Inconsistent cadence.** Posting daily for a month then disappearing kills momentum. Choose a sustainable rhythm.
- **Ignoring platform-native formats.** Cross-posting the same square image everywhere signals low effort to both algorithms and audiences.
- **No link strategy.** Every profile should route traffic somewhere useful; every promotional post should have a clear next step.
- **Engagement bait.** "Comment YES if you agree" tactics erode trust and get penalized. Earn engagement with genuinely good content.
- **Deleting underperforming posts.** It skews analytics and looks insecure. Leave them; learn from them.
- **Inconsistent brand voice across managers.** Multiple people posting in different tones. A one-page voice guide plus example posts prevents this.
- **Platform monoculture.** All effort on one platform. Algorithm changes or bans devastate single-platform strategies — diversify.
- **Ignoring DMs.** Treating social as broadcast only. DMs are customer service — staff them with SLAs.
