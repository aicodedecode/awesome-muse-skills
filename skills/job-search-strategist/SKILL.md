## Overview

A job search is a sales pipeline, and most candidates run it like a lottery — spraying applications
and hoping. This skill builds a systematic search: a sharply defined target list, a multi-channel
outreach plan (applications, referrals, recruiters, direct), and a weekly cadence with metrics. The
goal is a predictable flow of real conversations, not a pile of submitted applications.

## When to use

- Starting a job search from zero

- A search that's stalled (applications going nowhere)

- Running a confidential search while employed

- Targeting a specific tier (FAANG, startups, remote-first)

- Re-entering the market after a break

## Core concepts

- - **The funnel math.** Typical conversion: ~100 targeted applications → 10-15 screens → 3-5 loops
  → 1-2 offers. If your numbers are far worse, the problem is targeting or materials, not volume.
  Track it.
- - **Channels ranked by ROI.** Warm referral > cold outreach to hiring manager > recruiter
  (inbound) > direct application > job board spray. Allocate time accordingly: referrals deserve the
  most effort per unit.
- - **The target list.** 30-50 companies in tiers: 10 dream, 20 solid fits, 10-20 safety/practice.
  Research each enough to write one genuine sentence about why them. Practice interviews at tier 3
  before tier 1.
- - **Time-boxing.** A full-time search is a 30-40 hr/week job; an employed search is 5-10 hrs.
  Either way, schedule it like work — same hours, same place, metrics reviewed weekly.
- - **The follow-up rule.** Most responses come from follow-ups, not first touches. One polite bump
  after 5-7 days doubles reply rates. Silence is usually busyness, not rejection.
- - **Negotiation starts at application.** Every form field (salary expectations, notice period) is
  a negotiation input. This skill coordinates with salary-negotiator for the offer stage.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. **Define the ICP (ideal company profile).** Role, seniority, company stage/size, industry,
   location/remote, compensation floor, and 3 non-negotiables. Write it down — vague searches
   produce vague results.
2. 2. **Build the target list.** 30-50 companies with a named reason for each. Find the hiring
   manager or relevant team lead on LinkedIn for tier 1-2 companies.
3. 3. **Fix the materials first.** Tailored resume and LinkedIn (use resume-writer and
   linkedin-optimizer) before any outreach. A broken funnel wastes every lead.
4. 4. **Set up tracking.** Simple spreadsheet or board: company, role, contact, channel, date
   applied, stage, next action, notes. Review weekly; stale rows get a follow-up or a kill decision.
5. **Launch three channels in parallel.**

   - Referrals: message 2nd-degree connections with a short, specific ask (script provided below).

   -    - Direct: cold outreach to hiring managers — 3 sentences, one proof point, one ask for 15
     minutes.
   - Applications: tailored resume + cover letter where it matters; batch the rest.

6. 6. **Run the weekly review.** 30 minutes: funnel numbers, what's converting, what's stuck, next
   week's top 10 actions. Adjust the ICP if data says so (e.g., zero replies at Staff level → test
   Senior).
7. 7. **Prepare the close.** Interview prep (interview-coach), offer evaluation framework, and
   negotiation plan before offers arrive — not after.

**Referral ask script (adapt freely):**
"Hi [Name], I saw [Company] is hiring a [Role] on [Team] — it's squarely in my wheelhouse ([one-line
proof]). Would you be open to a brief chat about the team, or pointing me to the right person? Happy
to return the favor anytime."

## Common pitfalls

- - **Spray and pray.** 200 generic applications with a 1% response rate feels productive and isn't.
  Ten tailored, multi-channel approaches beat a hundred clicks.
- - **No tracking.** Without a pipeline view, follow-ups die and patterns stay invisible. The
  spreadsheet is non-negotiable.
- - **Applying before materials are ready.** Every weak application burns a 6-12 month
  re-application window at that company.
- - **Ignoring the hidden market.** Many roles are filled before posting. If 100% of your effort is
  job boards, you're fishing where everyone fishes.
- - **Neglecting employed-search discipline.** "I'll do it after work" without scheduled hours means
  it never happens. Calendar blocks or it doesn't exist.
- - **Taking rejection personally.** A "no" usually means fit, timing, or headcount — not worth.
  Debrief, adjust, move on. The funnel needs volume plus targeting, not perfection.
