# Task Manager

## Overview

Tools don't manage tasks; systems do. Pick the workflow before the app — always in that order.

Every effective system answers five questions: where do I capture, how do I clarify, where does it live, when do I review, what do I do now?

Task management fails for four predictable reasons: slow capture, vague tasks, flat lists with no priorities, and no reviews.

Fix those four and almost any tool works — from a $10/month app to index cards.

Mental shift: your system exists to let you forget. If tasks live in your head 'just in case,' the system has failed.

## When to use

- Choosing between task apps (or deciding you don't need a new one)
- Your current system feels overwhelming, stale, or untrusted
- Setting up task management for a team, family, or household
- Designing the workflow before picking the tool
- Evaluating whether a fancy new app will actually help (it won't, without the system)

## Core concepts

- **The five stages.**
  Capture -> Clarify -> Organize -> Review -> Engage. Every system implements these; most failures are a skipped stage.
- **Trusted system.**
  Your brain must believe the system holds everything and gets reviewed. One complete system beats two half-systems.
- **Next actions.**
  Every project needs a defined next physical, visible action. 'Plan trip' paralyzes; 'Email Maya for hotel recs' moves.
- **Horizons of focus.**
  Runway (today), projects (month), goals (year), vision (3-5 years). Review each at its own cadence: daily, weekly, quarterly, yearly.
- **Timeboxing.**
  A to-do list tells you what; a calendar tells you when. Assign tasks to blocks — the combination is what gets things done.
- **The 2-minute rule.**
  If clarifying reveals a sub-2-minute task, do it now. Filing a 90-second task costs more than doing it.
- **Waiting-for tracking.**
  Delegated items need a home with a follow-up date or they silently die. Waiting-for is a first-class list.
- **Someday/maybe parking lot.**
  Low-priority ideas need deliberate parking, reviewed monthly — otherwise they clog active lists or get lost.

## Practical workflow

1. **Pick one capture tool.**
   The one you'll actually open everywhere: app, notebook, voice memos. Speed and ubiquity beat features.
2. **Define your lists.**
   Minimum viable: Inbox, Next Actions, Waiting For, Projects, Someday/Maybe. Add contexts only if useful.
3. **Clarify ruthlessly.**
   Per inbox item: what's the outcome? Next physical action? <2 min (do it), delegatable (waiting-for + date), multi-step (project + next action).
4. **Organize by outcome.**
   Group by project/goal. Due dates only for real deadlines — fake urgency trains you to ignore all dates.
5. **Timebox the day.**
   Each morning place 3-5 key tasks into calendar blocks. Protect one 90-minute deep-work block. Leave 30% buffer.
6. **Do the weekly review.**
   Inbox to zero, every project checked for a next action, calendar scanned ahead. 30-60 min, same slot weekly.
7. **Review horizons quarterly.**
   Check projects against goals and vision. Kill or pause what no longer serves. Strategy meets the task list here.
8. **Keep one system.**
   Resist the second app, the side spreadsheet, the sticky notes. One system, fully captured, or the brain won't let go.

## Common pitfalls

- **Tool-hopping.**
  Switching apps monthly resets system and habits. Commit to one tool for a full quarter before judging it.
- **Vague tasks.**
  'Website', 'taxes', 'mom' are anxiety with a checkbox. Rewrite until the next action is physical and visible.
- **Over-scheduling.**
  10 hours of tasks in an 8-hour day. Leave 30-40% buffer or the plan collapses by noon.
- **Skipping the review.**
  A system without review rots in two weeks. The review isn't maintenance — the review IS the system.
- **Confusing projects and actions.**
  Multi-step projects in daily lists paralyze. Daily lists hold only next actions; projects live one level up.
- **No someday/maybe.**
  Without a parking lot, low-priority ideas clog active lists or get lost forever.
- **Head-storage.**
  'I'll remember' is the most expensive strategy ever invented. Not captured in 10 seconds = doesn't exist.
- **All capture, no clarify.**
  A 300-item inbox isn't a system, it's a junk drawer. Clarifying is the work; capturing is just intake.
