## Overview

Influencer marketing borrows trust: creators' audiences believe them, and that belief transfers to your brand when the fit is right. This skill covers the full process — defining goals, finding the right creators, outreach that gets replies, fair negotiation, campaign execution, and measuring real ROI beyond vanity metrics.

Fit beats fame. A micro-creator your buyers trust outperforms a celebrity they don't.

## When to use

- Launching an influencer program
- Finding creators for a campaign
- Writing outreach messages
- Negotiating rates and deliverables
- Managing an always-on creator roster
- Measuring influencer ROI

- Seeding products to creators before launch
- Building an always-on ambassador program
- Running whitelisted creator ads
- Launching in new geographic markets via local creators
- Driving app installs or signups at scale
- Building long-term brand affinity with Gen Z audiences
## Core concepts

**Creator tiers.** Nano (1–10k: highest engagement, cheapest), micro (10–100k: the sweet spot for most brands), macro (100k–1M), mega/celebrity (1M+: reach, low trust transfer). Match tier to goal: conversions → nano/micro; awareness → macro/mega.

**Fit criteria.** Audience overlap (their followers = your buyers), content alignment (would your product appear naturally?), engagement quality (comments showing real influence, not bots), brand safety (past content you'd stand behind), and authenticity (do they already use things like yours?).

**Compensation models.** Flat fee, gifting/product seeding, affiliate/commission, hybrid (fee + performance bonus), equity (rare, early-stage). Pay for deliverables and usage rights explicitly — assumptions cause disputes.

**Briefing.** Give creators the goal, key messages, must-haves (disclosure, links, tags), and don'ts — then let them create in their voice. Over-scripted creator content performs worse than authentic content. Review for accuracy, not style.

**FTC/regulatory disclosure.** Paid partnerships must be clearly disclosed (#ad, "paid partnership" labels). This is law in most markets, not etiquette. Brief every creator on it.

**Measurement.** Track with unique codes/links per creator: reach, engagement, clicks, conversions, CAC vs. other channels, and content reuse value (whitelisting/usage rights for ads often exceeds the original post's value).


**Whitelisting (paid amplification).** Running ads through creators' handles with their content — typically outperforms brand-handle ads on trust and CTR. Negotiate whitelisting rights upfront (duration, spend caps); it is often the highest-ROI part of the deal.

**Gifting vs. paid.** Gifting (free product, no obligations) works for genuinely loved products with creators who post organically; paid partnerships guarantee deliverables and messaging control. Match the model to the goal — gifting for discovery, paid for campaigns.

**Contract essentials.** Deliverables (format, count, timeline) → usage rights (duration, channels, paid amplification) → exclusivity (category, duration) → FTC disclosure requirements → payment terms (50/50 or on delivery) → kill clauses (brand safety violations).
Verbal agreements with creators end in disputes — written terms protect both sides.
**Content co-creation.** Briefs should define the guardrails (key messages, must-avoids, disclosure) and free the creator within them (format, humor, style).
Over-scripted creator content performs worse than brand ads — audiences smell inauthenticity instantly.
Review for compliance and accuracy, not for creative control.
**Measurement framework.** Awareness (reach, views, share of voice) → consideration (engagement, saves, site traffic) → conversion (code/link sales, signups) → long-term (brand lift studies, follower quality).
Match metrics to campaign goals upfront — awareness campaigns judged on sales will always "fail."
## Practical workflow

1. **Set goals and budget.** Awareness, content generation, conversions, or community? Budget determines tier mix. A common split: 70% micro/nano for conversions, 30% macro for reach.
2. **Find creators.** Search natively on platforms (hashtags, competitor mentions), use discovery tools, ask customers who they follow, check who already posts about your category. Build a longlist of 3–5x what you need.
3. **Vet thoroughly.** Audit: audience demographics, engagement rate vs. tier benchmarks, comment quality (real conversation vs. emoji spam), posting consistency, past brand deals (competitors? quality?), follower growth patterns (spikes suggest bought followers).
4. **Outreach personally.** Short message: why them specifically (prove you watched their content), the opportunity, what's in it for them, simple next step. Follow up once. Mass DMs get ignored.
5. **Negotiate and contract.** Scope: deliverables (format, quantity, timeline), usage rights (duration, channels — whitelisting costs extra), exclusivity (category, duration), payment terms, FTC disclosure requirement, kill fee. Get it in writing.
6. **Launch and manage.** Send product + brief, set check-ins, review drafts for factual accuracy, coordinate posting schedule. Support creators during the campaign — answer questions fast.
7. **Measure and nurture.** Report per-creator ROI. Turn winners into long-term ambassadors (ongoing relationships beat one-off posts). Repurpose top content as ads with permission.

**Outreach template skeleton:** specific compliment (video/post reference) → who you are (one line) → the idea (why it's a fit for their audience) → the offer (compensation type) → easy CTA ("open to a 15-min chat?").


**Creator vetting scorecard:** audience fit (1–5) → engagement rate vs. tier benchmark → comment authenticity (real conversation?) → content quality and consistency → brand safety (past 6 months scan) → past brand deals (competitors? performance?) → growth trend (organic vs. spikes). Minimum 18/30 to proceed.

**Outreach sequence:** research and shortlist (fit first, followers second) → personalized pitch (reference their actual content) → negotiate terms → ship product with creative brief → review draft (24h SLA) → launch coordination → amplify winners with paid → pay promptly → nurture relationships (top creators become ongoing partners).
Response rates run 10–20% for personalized outreach versus <2% for mass blasts.
**Campaign timeline:** 6–8 weeks for major campaigns (sourcing → negotiation → creation → review → launch).
Rush timelines produce rushed content — plan creator campaigns like the productions they are.
## Common pitfalls

- **Follower count worship.** Big audiences with no trust don't convert. Engagement quality and audience fit matter more.
- **Skipping vetting.** Fake followers, wrong audience, brand-unsafe history — discovered after payment. Audit before contracting.
- **Over-scripting creators.** Killing the authenticity you paid for. Brief the what, free the how.
- **No usage rights.** Great creator content you can't reuse in ads. Negotiate rights upfront — it's far cheaper than reshooting.
- **Ignoring disclosure rules.** Regulatory fines and trust damage. Brief and verify disclosure on every post.
- **One-off campaigns only.** Single posts spike and fade. Ambassador relationships compound.
- **Measuring likes.** Track conversions and CAC with unique codes. Likes don't pay bills.
- **Paying for posts, not outcomes.** No tracking codes or links. Every creator gets unique attribution — no exceptions.
- **Ignoring micro-creators.** Chasing follower counts while 10 micro-creators with true influence drive more conversions for less money.
- **Follower-count obsession.** Mega-influencers with disengaged audiences. Engagement rate and audience fit predict results; follower count predicts cost.
- **One-and-done campaigns.** Single posts that spike and vanish. Always-on creator relationships compound — 3–5 posts over months beat one splash.
