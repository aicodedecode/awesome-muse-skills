# Academy Guide

## Overview

Online courses and academies promise transformation; most deliver entertainment. The difference is the learner's method, not the platform.

Effective course learning: choose for outcomes not credentials, study actively (projects > videos), space and retrieve, and build proof of skill.

This skill covers selecting programs, studying them well, and converting completion into capability.

## When to use

- Choosing between courses, bootcamps, or academies
- Getting real value from an enrolled course (not just finishing)
- Learning a new professional skill systematically
- Evaluating whether a pricey program is worth it
- Turning course completion into career capability

## Core concepts

- **Outcome-first selection.**
  Define the skill and proof first ('build X,' 'pass Y'). Choose the program that produces that outcome fastest, not the most prestigious.
- **Project-based beats video-based.**
  Courses built around building/creating beat lecture marathons. Doing > watching, always. Check the syllabus for output, not hours.
- **Instructor credibility.**
  Practitioners with real results in the field beat professional course-sellers. Check what they've built, not just what they teach.
- **Reviews with outcomes.**
  Look for reviews citing specific results ('got the job,' 'shipped the project'), not vibes ('great instructor!').
- **Active studying.**
  Pause and practice, take notes in your words, do every exercise. Passive watching has ~5% retention; active practice has ~75%.
- **Spaced retrieval.**
  Revisit key concepts on expanding intervals; self-test instead of re-watching. Flashcards or practice problems for factual material.
- **Build in public.**
  Share projects and learnings as you go. Public building creates accountability, feedback, and proof of skill simultaneously.
- **Completion is not competence.**
  Certificates signal effort, not ability. The portfolio project / exam pass / shipped work is the real credential.

## Practical workflow

1. **Define the outcome.**
   Write: 'After this, I will be able to ___' and 'proof will be ___.' If you can't fill these, don't enroll yet.
2. **Vet the program.**
   Syllabus for projects/output, instructor track record, outcome-based reviews, refund policy. Talk to 2-3 alumni if expensive.
3. **Set the schedule.**
   Block realistic weekly hours on the calendar. 'When I have time' means never. Cohort courses: attend live; self-paced: set deadlines.
4. **Study actively.**
   Every session: do the exercises, build the project, write notes in your words. Never just watch — pause and practice constantly.
5. **Build the proof project.**
   Beyond coursework: one substantial project applying the skill to your context. This is what employers/clients actually evaluate.
6. **Space and retrieve.**
   Weekly self-tests on key concepts; revisit weak areas. Re-watching lectures is the least effective review method.
7. **Share progress.**
   Post learnings and project milestones. Feedback accelerates; public commitment sustains.
8. **Convert to capability.**
   After completion: apply the skill in real work within 30 days, or it evaporates. Schedule the application before finishing.

## Common pitfalls

- **Credential collecting.**
  Stacking certificates with no applied skill. Employers buy capability; certificates are tiebreakers at best.
- **Tutorial hell.**
  Course after course, project after project guided step-by-step, never building alone. At some point: no tutorial, just build.
- **Passive watching.**
  Treating courses like Netflix. Completion rates are low and retention lower — active practice is the whole game.
- **No outcome defined.**
  Enrolling because 'it seems useful.' Vague goals produce vague learning. Define the skill and proof first.
- **Expensive = better fallacy.**
  Price correlates with marketing, not outcomes. Vet by syllabus, instructor results, and alumni outcomes.
- **Skipping the exercises.**
  'I'll do the project later.' Later never comes. The exercises ARE the course; videos are the packaging.
- **Learning without applying.**
  Finishing a course with no plan to use the skill. Knowledge unused for 30 days might as well not exist.
- **No vetting.**
  Enrolling on ad copy alone. For anything over a few hundred dollars: syllabus deep-dive + alumni conversations.
