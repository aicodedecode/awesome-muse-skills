## Overview

Networking has a sleazy reputation because most people only do it transactionally — appearing when
they need a job. Done right, it's just building genuine professional relationships before you need
them. This skill covers outreach that gets replies, follow-up that builds real connection, and event
strategies for introverts and extroverts alike.

## When to use

- Building a network from scratch in a new industry or city

- Reaching out to people at target companies

- Preparing for conferences, meetups, or industry events

- Reconnecting with dormant contacts

- Building a habit of consistent, low-effort networking

## Core concepts

- - **Give before you ask.** Share an article, make an intro, offer feedback — before ever
  requesting anything. The ledger of goodwill is the whole game.
- - **Specificity gets replies.** "Can I pick your brain?" is a burden. "I'm deciding between two
  data engineering paths and your post on X was the clearest take I've seen — 15 minutes next week?"
  is easy to say yes to.
- - **The 5-minute favor.** Keep asks tiny: one question, one intro, one piece of feedback. Small
  asks get yeses; big asks get ignored.
- - **Follow-up is the relationship.** One meeting is an encounter; the relationship is built in the
  2-3 thoughtful follow-ups over the next year. Most people never follow up — that's your edge.
- - **Weak ties, strong results.** Acquaintances (not close friends) drive most job leads and
  opportunities. Breadth of genuine-but-light connections beats a tiny inner circle for career
  optionality.
- - **Document, don't rely on memory.** A simple CRM (even a spreadsheet): who, where you met, what
  you discussed, when to follow up. Memory fails; systems don't.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. **Set a sustainable cadence.** 2-3 outreach touches per week beats a monthly binge. Block 30
   minutes weekly — consistency compounds.
2. 2. **Build the target list.** 20-30 people: peers doing interesting work, seniors in your target
   roles, alumni, people whose writing you admire. Prioritize genuine curiosity over pure utility.
3. 3. **Write the outreach.** Three sentences: who you are (one line), why them specifically (prove
   you did homework), the tiny ask. Personalize the middle sentence — it's the only part that
   matters.
4. 4. **Make the conversation good.** Prepare 3 questions. Listen more than you talk. End with:
   "what's the most useful thing I could send your way?" and "is there anyone else you think I
   should talk to?"
5. 5. **Follow up within 48 hours.** Thank-you note with one specific thing you took away. Then add
   them to your light-touch system.
6. 6. **Run the light-touch system.** Quarterly: share something relevant to their interests,
   congratulate wins, comment thoughtfully on their posts. 5 minutes per person per quarter
   maintains the tie.
7. 7. **Work events deliberately.** Before: pick 3 people to meet, prepare one good question each.
   During: aim for 3-5 real conversations, not 30 business cards. After: follow up within 48 hours
   referencing something specific you discussed.

**Outreach template:**
"Hi [Name], I'm [one-line context]. Your [specific work/post/talk] on [topic] really resonated —
especially [one detail]. I'm [your specific situation/question]. Would you be open to a 15-min chat
next week? No worries if timing's bad."

## Common pitfalls

- - **Only networking when job hunting.** Desperation is detectable. The network you need in a
  crisis is the one you built when you didn't need it.
- - **Generic mass outreach.** "I'd love to connect" with no context gets ignored. The specific
  middle sentence is the whole message.
- - **Making it all about you.** Conversations where you only talk about your needs feel extractive.
  Lead with curiosity about them.
- - **Never following up.** 90% of networking value is in follow-up, and 90% of people skip it. A
  simple system beats good intentions.
- - **Collecting contacts, not relationships.** 500 shallow connections lose to 50 real ones. Depth
  in a few beats breadth with none.
- - **Forgetting to close the loop.** If someone helps you, tell them the outcome. "Your intro led
  to an offer — thank you" turns a favor into a lasting ally.
