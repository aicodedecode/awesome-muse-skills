# Readwise Pro

## Overview

Readwise collects highlights from Kindle, articles, PDFs, and more into one library — then resurfaces them via spaced repetition.

The premise: highlights are worthless if never reviewed. Daily review emails/app sessions turn passive highlighting into retained knowledge.

Pro usage: clean capture pipeline, disciplined daily review, and exporting the best highlights into your permanent notes with your own commentary.

## When to use

- Reading heavily on Kindle, web articles, or PDFs
- Highlights scattered across apps, never reviewed
- Building a personal knowledge base from reading
- Studying or researching with many sources
- Wanting spaced repetition applied to your own reading

## Core concepts

- **Unified highlight inbox.**
  One library ingesting from all sources: Kindle, browser extension, PDFs, even Twitter/X threads. Capture everywhere, review in one place.
- **Spaced repetition review.**
  Daily resurfaced highlights on an expanding schedule. The algorithm handles timing; your job is showing up and engaging.
- **Review as thinking.**
  Don't just skim — favorite, tag, comment, delete. Active engagement during review is what converts highlights to knowledge.
- **Tagging taxonomy.**
  A few consistent tags (project names, topics, 'quote', 'idea') make highlights retrievable later. Tag during review, not capture.
- **Reader (read-later).**
  Readwise's Reader app: save articles, highlight natively, everything flows to review. Replaces scattered read-later apps.
- **Export pipeline.**
  Best highlights flow to your notes app (Notion, Obsidian, etc.) with source metadata. Reviewed -> exported -> permanent notes.
- **Highlight discipline.**
  Highlight less, better. A page of yellow teaches nothing; one sharp highlight per chapter teaches plenty.
- **Deletion as curation.**
  Delete mediocre highlights during review without mercy. A curated library of 500 great highlights beats 5,000 mediocre ones.

## Practical workflow

1. **Connect all sources.**
   Kindle, browser extension, PDF app, read-later. Verify highlights actually flow — test each source once.
2. **Set up Reader.**
   Make it the default read-later: save from anywhere, highlight natively, triage the queue weekly.
3. **Configure daily review.**
   Pick the delivery (email or app), set count (10-20/day), choose the time. Daily consistency beats volume.
4. **Review actively.**
   Each highlight: favorite/tag/comment or delete. Add one line in your own words to the keepers — that's the learning.
5. **Build tag taxonomy.**
   5-10 tags max: projects, key topics, 'quote', 'to-write-about'. Apply during review sessions.
6. **Export weekly.**
   Top highlights -> notes app with source links. Convert the best into permanent notes in your own words.
7. **Prune the library.**
   Monthly: delete stale highlights, merge tags, archive finished books. Curation is continuous.
8. **Track reading stats.**
   Review streaks and volume for motivation, but judge by exported notes — output, not input.

## Common pitfalls

- **Highlighting everything.**
  Yellow-drenched pages with no discrimination. Highlight the surprising, the quotable, the actionable — not the obvious.
- **Skipping daily review.**
  Capture without review is hoarding. The review is the product; everything else is plumbing.
- **Passive reviewing.**
  Skimming the email without engaging. Favorite, tag, comment, or delete — every highlight gets a decision.
- **No export pipeline.**
  Reviewed highlights dying in the app. The best must flow into permanent notes or the value evaporates.
- **Too many tags.**
  40 tags with one highlight each. Fewer, consistent tags or the taxonomy is decoration.
- **Read-later graveyard.**
  Saving 200 articles, reading 5. Triage the queue weekly; delete the aspirational backlog honestly.
- **Ignoring deletion.**
  Keeping every highlight 'just in case.' Curation requires killing — the library's value is its density.
- **Review bloat.**
  50 highlights a day becomes a chore you skip. 10-20 quality resurfaces sustain the habit for years.
