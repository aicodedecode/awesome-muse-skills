## Overview

Reviews are modern word-of-mouth: they drive local SEO, purchase decisions, and trust. This skill covers the full review practice — generating authentic reviews at scale, responding to positive and negative reviews, monitoring reputation across platforms, and using review insights to improve the business.


Reviews are the new word-of-mouth at scale: 90%+ of buyers read them, and star ratings directly move conversion. Review management is both defensive (responding to negatives, catching fake reviews) and offensive (generating authentic positives systematically). It is a compounding asset — every review works for you indefinitely.

## Core concepts

**The review funnel.** Ask → make it easy → follow up → respond → showcase. Most happy customers never review unless asked; most unhappy ones review unprompted. Systematic asking corrects the negativity bias.

**Asking well.** Timing (right after positive moments: delivery, successful support, 5-star NPS), channel (email, SMS, in-app, in-person with QR codes), and simplicity (direct links to the right platform, no multi-step processes). Never incentivize with payments for positive reviews — it's against most platforms' policies and often illegal; you can incentivize honest reviews in some contexts, but check platform rules first.

**Response playbook.** Positive: thank personally, mention specifics, invite back. Negative: respond quickly (24–48h), empathize publicly, take resolution offline, follow up publicly when resolved. Never argue, never be defensive, never fake.

**Platform strategy.** Prioritize where your customers look: Google (local SEO + trust), industry-specific (e.g., G2/Capterra for software, TripAdvisor for hospitality), then secondary. Claim and complete every profile — unclaimed profiles collect unanswered complaints.

**Reputation monitoring.** Alerts for new reviews and mentions, sentiment tracking over time, competitor review benchmarking. Speed matters — a negative review unanswered for weeks signals indifference.

**Reviews as assets.** Showcase top reviews on site/social/ads (with permission where needed), mine reviews for messaging (customer language is copy gold), and feed recurring complaints to product/ops as improvement priorities.


**The review request moment.** Ask when satisfaction peaks: after delivery confirmation, after a resolved support ticket, after a milestone achieved in-product. Never ask after a complaint or a failed interaction. In-product prompts at the moment of delight convert 5–10x better than post-purchase emails.

**Responding to negatives (the HEARD method):** Hear them out (acknowledge specifically) → Empathize (genuine, not scripted) → Apologize where warranted → Resolve (offer a path forward) → Diagnose (fix the root cause). A thoughtful response to a 1-star review influences every future reader more than the review itself.

**Platform strategy.** Google (dominant for local), Yelp (influential, filtered aggressively), Facebook (social proof), industry-specific (TripAdvisor, Healthgrades, G2).
Prioritize by where your customers actually decide — not all platforms matter equally per vertical.
Claim and complete profiles everywhere relevant; optimize effort toward the top 2–3.
**Review velocity.** Steady streams beat bursts: 5–10 new reviews monthly per location sustains rankings and trust.
Systematize asks (post-transaction triggers) rather than campaigning sporadically.
Monitor velocity per location; stalled locations need intervention.
**Sentiment analysis.** Categorize feedback themes (service, product, price, staff) → track trends → route insights to operations.
Reviews are free market research — aggregate them into operational improvements.
Share positive themes with teams; recognition reinforces what works.
## Practical workflow

1. **Audit.** Current ratings per platform, review velocity, response rate and time, sentiment themes, competitor comparison. Find the gaps.
2. **Claim and optimize profiles.** Every relevant platform: complete info, photos, accurate hours/services, correct categories. Unclaimed profiles are reputation liabilities.
3. **Build generation systems.** Post-transaction email/SMS sequences (ask happy customers at the right moment), in-store QR codes, staff training (ask in person — highest conversion), and review links in receipts/follow-ups. Target steady velocity, not bursts.
4. **Respond systematically.** Daily monitoring, response SLA (24–48h), templates as starting points (personalize every response), escalation path for serious complaints, and a process for flagging fake/defamatory reviews.
5. **Showcase and leverage.** Review widgets on site, top reviews in ads and social, case studies from detailed reviews, and "you asked, we fixed" posts when reviews drive improvements.
6. **Analyze and improve.** Monthly: rating trends, sentiment themes, response metrics, competitor movement. Quarterly: feed top complaint themes to operations with data.

**Negative review response template:** acknowledge specifically → empathize genuinely → take it offline (name + direct contact) → resolve → follow up publicly. "We're sorry to hear about [specific issue]. This isn't the experience we want. I'm [name], [title] — please reach me at [contact] so I can make this right."


**Review generation system:** map the customer journey → identify 2–3 peak-satisfaction moments → build asks into those moments (in-product, email, SMS) → make leaving a review frictionless (direct links, no account creation) → thank reviewers personally → showcase reviews in marketing. Measure: review velocity, average rating trend, response rate, and response time.

**Crisis protocol:** negative review velocity spike → assess validity (real issue or attack?) → respond publicly with HEARD → fix root cause → follow up privately → if fake/coordinated, report with evidence. Never offer incentives for removing negative reviews — it violates most platforms' policies.

**Multi-location program:** centralized platform (monitoring, response templates, analytics) → location-level ownership (managers respond within 24h) → escalation paths (serious complaints to senior staff) → monthly scorecards per location.
Balance central efficiency with local authenticity — templated responses to everything feel robotic.
**Viral incident response:** assess scope and validity → respond publicly (acknowledge, empathize, commit to action) → take detailed discussion private → implement fixes → follow up publicly with resolution.
Speed matters, but accuracy matters more — verify facts before responding publicly.
## Common pitfalls

- **Never asking.** Relying on organic reviews guarantees negativity bias. Ask systematically.
- **Fake or incentivized positives.** Platform penalties, legal risk, and destroyed trust when exposed. Earn reviews honestly.
- **Arguing with reviewers.** Public defensiveness amplifies damage. Empathize and move offline.
- **Ignoring reviews.** Unanswered reviews — especially negative — signal you don't care. Respond to all negative, many positive.
- **Review gating.** Only soliciting from happy customers (against most platforms' policies). Ask everyone; fix what you learn.
- **Burst campaigns.** 50 reviews in a week then silence looks manipulated. Steady velocity wins.
- **Not acting on feedback.** Reviews revealing the same complaint for months with no fix. Close the loop operationally.
- **Incentivizing only positive reviews.** Asking only happy customers skews ratings and violates FTC/platform rules. Ask everyone; earn the positives.
- **Ignoring reviews.** Unanswered negatives signal indifference. Respond to 100% of negatives and a sample of positives.
- **Fighting fake reviews emotionally.** Public accusations backfire. Report through proper channels with documentation; respond professionally regardless.
- **Fake review panic.** Obsessing over competitors' suspicious reviews. Report with evidence, then focus on earning genuine reviews — authenticity wins long-term.
- **Ignoring positive reviews.** Only responding to negatives. Thanking positive reviewers builds loyalty and encourages more reviews.
