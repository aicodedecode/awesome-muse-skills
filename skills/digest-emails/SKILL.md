## Overview

Digest emails (daily/weekly summaries) keep users informed without real-time interruption: activity summaries, content roundups, and team updates. This skill covers designing digests people actually open — curation, personalization, frequency, and the engagement mechanics that prevent the digest from becoming inbox wallpaper.


Digest emails — daily/weekly summaries of activity, content, or updates — are the highest-retention email format for platforms: they deliver ongoing value in one predictable package, drive habitual re-engagement, and are far less annoying than per-event notifications. The skill is curation: making each edition feel personally relevant rather than automated noise.
## When to use

- Building daily/weekly digest emails
- Improving digest open and click rates
- Reducing notification email volume via digests
- Designing personalized content roundups
- Launching team/company update emails
- Fixing digest fatigue

- Re-engaging dormant users with activity summaries
- Curating content newsletters
- Replacing noisy per-event emails
- Summarizing community or forum activity
- Delivering personalized learning paths
## Core concepts

**Curation over completeness.** A digest is an editorial product: the 5–10 most important items, not everything that happened. Rank by: relevance to the recipient, importance, recency, and actionability. "Everything" belongs in the app; the digest carries what matters.

**Personalization.** By role, interests, behavior, and relationships (updates from people you work with rank higher). Even simple personalization (name, relevant items first) lifts engagement significantly. The best digests feel hand-picked.

**Frequency strategy.** Daily for fast-moving contexts (sales activity, support queues), weekly for most content/updates, and user choice where possible. Match frequency to content velocity — a weekly digest with 2 items feels empty; a daily with 50 feels like spam.

**Anatomy.** Subject line (specific + fresh: "3 updates need your input" beats "Your weekly digest"), personalized greeting optional, top story featured, grouped sections with clear headers, one-line summaries per item (not just links), single primary CTA per section, and footer with preference controls.

**Engagement mechanics.** "What you missed" framing, social proof ("most discussed"), unfinished business (items awaiting your action — highest CTR), and freshness (never repeat yesterday's content). Every digest should contain at least one item worth clicking.

**Consolidation.** Digests should replace individual notification emails, not add to them. When launching a digest, turn off the real-time emails it covers (with user control). Net email reduction is the goal.


**Curation algorithms.** Rank candidate items by: personal relevance (involves the user or their network), recency, popularity/social proof, and diversity (not 10 items from one source). The top 5–7 items make the digest; everything else waits. Personalization depth separates beloved digests (GitHub, Strava) from ignored ones — invest in the ranking, not just the template.

**Cadence and control.** Daily for high-activity products, weekly for most, with user-selectable frequency. The frequency selector is itself a retention feature — users who choose weekly over unsubscribing are saved. Default new users to the gentlest effective cadence; let engagement earn frequency.

**The subject line.** Digests live or die by subject lines: include the most compelling item ("Sarah mentioned you + 4 updates") not generic labels ("Your weekly digest"). Dynamic subject lines reflecting top content lift open rates 15–25%.

**Send-time personalization.** Deliver when each user typically engages — morning readers vs. evening readers.
Time-zone-aware batching prevents 3am digests.
Test send times per cohort; defaults are averages that fit nobody.
**Content freshness.** Digests must feel current — stale items signal irrelevance.
Set recency cutoffs (nothing older than 7 days for weeklies).
 evergreen content gets its own format, not digest filler.
## Practical workflow

1. **Define the digest's job.** What should the reader know/do after reading? One clear purpose per digest. If you can't state it, don't send it.
2. **Design curation rules.** Scoring: recency × relevance × importance × actionability. Minimum quality bar (don't send thin digests), maximum items (5–10), and deduplication against previous digests.
3. **Build personalization.** Start with: role-based sections, behavioral ranking (their projects, their mentions first), and preference inputs. Increase sophistication as data allows.
4. **Write the template.** Scannable sections, one-line summaries, clear CTAs, mobile-first design (most digests are read on phones), and prominent preference/unsubscribe controls.
5. **Launch and consolidate.** Ship the digest, migrate users off redundant real-time emails, communicate the change ("fewer emails, better summaries"), and offer frequency choice.
6. **Optimize.** Track: open rate, CTR per section, items-per-open, unsubscribe rate, and preference changes. A/B test subject lines and section order. Prune sections nobody clicks. Survey annually: still useful?

**Curation scoring example:** score = (recency decay) × (relationship weight: direct mention 3x, team 2x, org 1x) × (actionability: needs-you 2x) — take top N above threshold.


**Digest anatomy:** personalized greeting → hero item (the single most relevant update) → 4–6 ranked items with rich previews → one clear CTA per item (not a generic "view all") → footer with frequency controls. Mobile-first design — 60%+ of digest opens are mobile. Keep total length scannable in under 60 seconds.

**Optimization loop:** track open rate, click-through per position (position decay reveals ranking quality), unsubscribe rate, and re-activation (dormant users returning). A/B test: subject line formulas, item count, send day/time, and hero selection logic. Quarterly: survey a sample on relevance — quantitative metrics miss the "this feels generic" problem.
## Common pitfalls

- **Completeness over curation.** Dumping everything. Digests are editorial — curate ruthlessly.
- **Stale content.** Repeating items across digests. Track what's been sent; never repeat.
- **Adding without subtracting.** New digest on top of existing notification emails. Consolidate — net reduction.
- **Generic for everyone.** Same digest for all roles. Personalize or perish (in the spam folder).
- **Weak subject lines.** "Your weekly digest" every week. Specific, fresh subjects referencing actual content.
- **No preference control.** Forcing frequency. Let users choose daily/weekly/off.
- **Ignoring mobile.** Desktop-designed digests read on phones. Mobile-first, always.
- **Kitchen-sink digests.** Including everything to avoid choosing. Curation is the product — if everything is included, nothing is curated.
- **Stale personalization.** "Recommended for you" that clearly is not. Bad personalization is worse than none — it signals you do not know the user.
- **No sunset for non-openers.** Continuing daily digests to users who never open. Suppress or downshift chronic non-openers to protect deliverability.
- **Identical digests for all.** No personalization beyond the name. Segment by interest or behavior — relevance is the product.
- **Broken links.** Digest links rotting. Validate links before sending; broken links train users to stop clicking.
