# Calendar Pro

## Overview

Your calendar is the only productivity tool with a hard constraint: time. Task lists are advice; the calendar is commitment.

Four parts: structure (layers for work/personal/focus), timeboxing (work assigned to blocks), meeting hygiene, and rituals.

Done right, the calendar becomes a realistic weekly plan — not a graveyard of intentions and other people's priorities.

Every important task gets a block. Meetings earn their slots. Buffers absorb reality.

## When to use

- Rebuilding a chaotic, overfull schedule
- Protecting focus time in a meeting-heavy role
- Designing team meeting norms and calendar etiquette
- Setting up shared family or team calendars
- Moving from reactive days to deliberately planned weeks

## Core concepts

- **Timebox everything important.**
  No block = probably won't happen. Deep work, admin, email windows, exercise, downtime — all get blocks.
- **Calendar layers.**
  Separate calendars (work, personal, focus, team) toggled as needed. Color by energy/type, not just by calendar.
- **Buffers and transitions.**
  5-15 min between meetings, travel time where needed, ~30% daily slack. Back-to-back scheduling is fantasy.
- **Meeting defaults.**
  25/50 instead of 30/60, agendas required for meetings you own, recurring meetings re-earn slots quarterly.
- **Energy-matched scheduling.**
  Deep work in peak hours, meetings midday, admin in low-energy slots. Schedule with your biology.
- **The weekly plan.**
  20-30 min converting the task list into a realistic calendar. Without it, the week happens to you.
- **Daily shutdown.**
  10 min end-of-day: review tomorrow, prep materials, close loops. Tomorrow starts calm.
- **No-meeting blocks.**
  Reclaim at least one meeting-light day or daily focus block per week. Defend it like a client meeting.

## Practical workflow

1. **Audit the current calendar.**
   One honest week: meetings vs. focus vs. empty vs. overrun. Most discover meetings ate the week.
2. **Set up layers.**
   Work, Personal, Focus, optionally Team/OOO. Share selectively — free/busy is usually enough for others.
3. **Block non-negotiables first.**
   Deep work (2-4/week min), exercise, personal, weekly review — scheduled before meetings fill gaps.
4. **Fix meeting defaults.**
   Shorten default durations, enable auto-buffers, require agendas. Decline or shorten agenda-less invites.
5. **Run the weekly planning ritual.**
   Sunday or Monday, 20-30 min: review last week, place top 3-5 priorities in blocks, confirm buffers exist.
6. **Do the daily shutdown.**
   10 min: review tomorrow, prep, write down open loops so they leave your head. Then close the laptop.
7. **Protect and defend.**
   'I have a commitment then' is a complete sentence. Treat focus blocks like meetings with your best client.
8. **Review monthly.**
   Which blocks survived? Which got eaten, and by what? Adjust defenses; the calendar is a living system.

## Common pitfalls

- **Calendar as wish list.**
  12 hours of deep work daily in a meeting-heavy role is fantasy. 2-4 focused hours is an excellent day.
- **No buffers.**
  Back-to-back all day, then exhaustion and nothing else done. Transitions and overruns are real — schedule them.
- **Meetings without agendas.**
  No agenda, no meeting. This single rule kills a shocking fraction of bad meetings.
- **Letting others own your calendar.**
  Open booking links fill every gap. Use holdback blocks, office hours, and booking windows instead.
- **Ignoring energy.**
  Creative work at 3pm when you're sharpest at 9am. Track energy a week, then schedule around it.
- **Skipping the weekly plan.**
  A reactive calendar becomes everyone else's to-do list for your time. 25 min weekly is the highest-ROI habit.
- **Over-color-coding.**
  Twelve colors with a forgotten legend. Three to five meaningful colors max.
- **Fake no-meeting days.**
  Declaring focus days but accepting 'just one quick sync.' Defend the boundary or delete the label.
