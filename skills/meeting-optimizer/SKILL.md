# Meeting Optimizer

## Overview

Meetings are the most expensive communication: everyone's time simultaneously, plus prep and recovery.

Most run on autopilot: no agenda, wrong attendees, no decisions, no follow-through.

Optimize = question each meeting (async instead?), design survivors, facilitate tightly, record decisions.

Goal isn't zero meetings — it's meetings that earn their cost.

## When to use

- Team complains about too many meetings (or silently disengages in them)
- Meetings run over, drift, or end without decisions
- Designing meeting culture for a new team or company
- Deciding between a meeting and an async alternative
- Preparing to facilitate an important meeting well

## Core concepts

- **Purpose before scheduling.**
  Decide, align, create, or connect. 'Sync' isn't a purpose — what decision or output justifies gathering everyone?
- **The agenda is the meeting.**
  Topics, owner per topic, timeboxes, pre-reads — shared 24h ahead. No agenda = permission to decline.
- **Minimum viable attendees.**
  Roles: decider, contributor, informed. Informed people get notes, not invites. Easier to add than to un-waste an hour.
- **Async by default.**
  Status, FYI, approvals, doc feedback, first-draft brainstorming — all better async: written, on your schedule, with a record.
- **Decisions need records.**
  Close every meeting: what was decided, owner, date. Share within hours. Unrecorded decisions get re-litigated.
- **Timebox ruthlessly.**
  25/50 defaults, hard stops, visible parking lot. Ending 10 min early is a gift; overrun steals from the next commitment.
- **Pre-reads, not presentations.**
  Information transfer happens before, in writing. Meeting time is for questions, debate, decisions.
- **Facilitator + note-taker.**
  Separate roles, rotate them. Facilitator runs the agenda; note-taker captures decisions and actions live.

## Practical workflow

1. **Audit current meetings.**
   List recurring meetings: purpose, attendees, frequency, cost (people x time x freq). Score: keep / shorten / async / kill.
2. **Apply the async test.**
   'Could a doc + 48h of comments do this?' If yes, convert. Start with status meetings — almost always convertible.
3. **Template the survivors.**
   Decision meeting (options, recommendation, decision), weekly team (wins, priorities, blockers), 1:1 (their agenda first).
4. **Set facilitation norms.**
   Start on time regardless, timebox visibly, end 5 min early for action review. Rotate facilitation team-wide.
5. **Run tight meetings.**
   Open with purpose + desired outcome, work the agenda, park tangents visibly, close with decisions read back aloud.
6. **Follow through fast.**
   Notes within hours. Actions into task systems with dates — not left in the doc. Review open actions next time.
7. **Re-audit quarterly.**
   Every recurring meeting re-earns its slot. Kill liberally; calendars refill on their own if you let them.
8. **Default to shorter.**
   Change your defaults to 25/50. Most 60-min meetings fit in 50; most 30-min in 25. The constraint sharpens discussion.

## Common pitfalls

- **Status meetings as docs-in-disguise.**
  Written updates are faster to consume and searchable. The most convertible — and most common — waste.
- **Inviting everyone 'just in case'.**
  Twelve attendees, three talkers. Contributors get invites; everyone else gets notes.
- **No pre-reads.**
  Burning synchronous time on what could be read. Send materials early; start with questions.
- **Decision-free endings.**
  'Great discussion!' with no decision or owners. Even 'decide Tuesday with Sam's data' is a decision + owner + date.
- **Back-to-back scheduling.**
  No prep, debrief, or note capture. Buffers are hygiene, not luxury.
- **Recurring meeting inertia.**
  A 2022 weekly nobody would schedule today. Sunset it; let it be re-proposed with fresh purpose if needed.
- **Camera theater.**
  Mandating cameras without fixing purpose. Fix agenda and size first; engagement follows good design.
- **No written record.**
  Verbal decisions remembered differently by each attendee. Notes are the meeting's only durable output.
