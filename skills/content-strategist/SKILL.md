## Overview

Content strategy is the plan behind the publishing: who the content serves, what jobs it does, how it's produced and distributed, and how success is measured. This skill covers building a strategy from audience research through editorial operations to performance analysis — for blogs, video, podcasts, or mixed programs.

Strategy before production. Random acts of content waste budget; a strategy compounds.

## When to use

- Building a content program from scratch
- Fixing a blog or channel that publishes but doesn't perform
- Aligning content with product launches or campaigns
- Deciding what formats and channels to invest in
- Creating an editorial calendar the team can actually follow
- Justifying content budget with a measurement framework

- Merging content operations after an acquisition
- Deciding whether to invest in video vs. written content
- Building a thought-leadership program for executives
- Auditing content ROI across channels
- Building content operations for scale
- Aligning content with product launches
## Core concepts

**Audience and jobs-to-be-done.** Define 1–3 audience segments and the jobs content does for them (learn a skill, evaluate a vendor, stay current, get inspired). Every piece should serve a job for a segment — if it doesn't, cut it.

**Content pillars and themes.** 3–5 pillars derived from audience needs and business goals. Pillars organize ideation, keep coverage balanced, and make repurposing systematic.

**The content funnel.** TOFU (awareness: broad, educational), MOFU (consideration: comparisons, deep dives), BOFU (decision: case studies, demos, pricing guides). Map content to funnel stages and identify gaps — most teams overproduce TOFU and starve BOFU.

**Distribution > production.** Great content with no distribution dies. Plan distribution per piece: owned (email, site), earned (PR, partners, community), paid (boosts, syndication). The 80/20 rule: spend 20% creating, 80% distributing.

**Content operations.** Briefs, style guide, review workflow, publishing checklist, ownership (who writes, edits, approves, publishes). Strategy fails without operations.


**Content moats.** Durable advantages competitors cannot easily copy: proprietary data and research, genuine expertise and access, community-generated content, and distinctive formats or voices. Strategy should deliberately build at least one moat — otherwise you are competing on volume alone.

**Decay and refresh economics.** High-performing posts decay as information ages and competitors improve. Model content as an asset portfolio: 70% maintenance of winners, 30% new bets. A refresh (updating, expanding, re-promoting) often returns more per hour than a new post.

**Content pillars and themes.** 3–5 enduring pillars (e.g., for a fintech: money basics, investing, taxes, product education) that organize all production.
Pillars prevent random acts of content — every piece maps to a pillar and an audience need.
Review pillars annually; retire stale ones, promote emerging topics.
**Editorial calendar mechanics.** Planning horizon (quarterly themes, monthly focus, weekly execution) → intake process (who can request content?) → prioritization rubric → production workflow (brief → draft → review → publish → distribute) → performance review.
The calendar is a coordination tool, not a publishing schedule — its job is aligning teams, not filling slots.
**Distribution strategy.** Owned (blog, email, social) → earned (PR, partnerships, SEO) → paid (amplification of proven winners).
Create once, distribute natively per channel — not cross-posted identically.
Spend 50% of effort on distribution; great content with no distribution is a diary entry.
## Practical workflow

1. **Research.** Audit existing content (traffic, conversions, gaps). Interview customers and sales about questions prospects ask. Analyze competitors' top content and find underserved angles.
2. **Define strategy.** Document: audience segments + jobs, pillars, funnel map, channel priorities, voice/tone notes, and 3–5 strategic bets (e.g., "own the comparison-keyword cluster").
3. **Build the editorial calendar.** Quarterly themes → monthly focus → weekly slots. Each item: working title, pillar, funnel stage, format, owner, due date, distribution plan, CTA. Keep 20% capacity for reactive content.
4. **Create the production system.** Standard brief template, style guide, review stages (draft → edit → fact-check → approve), and a definition of done. Set realistic cadence — consistency beats bursts.
5. **Repurpose systematically.** Every major piece becomes: social posts, email, short video, quote graphics, and a discussion prompt. Plan repurposing at briefing time, not after publishing.
6. **Measure and review.** Monthly: traffic, engagement, leads/sales influenced, content efficiency (output per effort). Quarterly: pillar performance, funnel gaps, kill/keep decisions on underperforming series.

**Strategy one-pager:** audience, goals, pillars, funnel focus, channels, cadence, 3 strategic bets, success metrics. If it doesn't fit one page, it's not clear enough.


**Quarterly content portfolio review:** classify every major asset as grow (invest more), maintain (refresh on schedule), or kill (sunset and redirect). Reallocate effort from the bottom quartile to the top. This prevents the slow accumulation of mediocre content.

**Content brief template:** objective (one) → audience (one persona) → key message (one sentence) → CTA (one action) → format and channel → SEO requirements (if applicable) → SME and reviewer → deadline → success metric.
Briefs prevent the #1 content failure: producing something nobody asked for that serves no goal.
**Repurposing workflow:** pillar asset (research report, webinar) → atomize into 10+ derivatives (blog posts, social threads, videos, infographics, email series, sales one-pagers).
Plan atomization before creating the pillar — capture video, quotes, and data in formats the derivatives need.
## Common pitfalls

- **No documented strategy.** "Publish more" is not a strategy. Write it down or it doesn't exist.
- **Publishing for the algorithm, not the audience.** Chasing trends without serving customer jobs builds empty traffic.
- **BOFU neglect.** Awareness content is fun to make; decision-stage content closes deals. Balance the funnel.
- **Inconsistent cadence.** Sporadic publishing trains audiences to ignore you. Pick a sustainable pace.
- **Skipping distribution.** Hitting publish and moving on wastes the production investment. Distribution is a planned step.
- **Measuring vanity.** Pageviews without business impact don't justify budget. Track pipeline and revenue influence.
- **No kill criteria.** Keep producing losing formats out of habit. Review quarterly and cut what doesn't work.
- **Founder-bottleneck content.** Strategy depending on one executive's time for every piece. Build systems (interviews-to-draft, ghostwriting) that scale beyond individuals.
- **No content retirement.** Keeping outdated, off-brand, or underperforming content live. Prune annually — it lifts the average quality of everything else.
- **Channel-first thinking.** "We need a TikTok strategy" before knowing the message. Message and audience first; channels are delivery vehicles.
- **No distribution budget.** Spending 100% on creation, 0% on distribution. Even great content needs initial push — reserve 30–50% for amplification.
