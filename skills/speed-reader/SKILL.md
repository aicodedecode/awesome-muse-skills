# Speed Reader

## Overview

Reading faster isn't one skill — it's choosing the right speed for the purpose: skimming for relevance, scanning for facts, deep reading for understanding.

Most slow reading comes from bad habits (subvocalization, regression) and wrong strategy (reading everything at the same speed).

Realistic gains: 2-3x for skimming/scanning with practice; deep reading speed improves modestly — comprehension is the constraint.

## When to use

- Keeping up with heavy reading loads (study, research, work)
- Triaging articles, papers, and reports efficiently
- Exam preparation with large syllabi
- Professionals drowning in required reading
- Distinguishing what deserves deep reading from what doesn't

## Core concepts

- **Purpose-first.**
  Before reading: what do I need from this? Gist, specific facts, deep understanding? Purpose determines speed and strategy.
- **Previewing.**
  Read title, abstract, headings, first/last paragraphs first. Build a mental map; decide if deep reading is warranted.
- **Skimming.**
  Fast pass for gist: headings, topic sentences, visuals, conclusions. 3-5x normal speed, 60-70% comprehension of structure.
- **Scanning.**
  Hunting specific information: names, dates, figures. Eyes move in patterns (Z or F); ignore everything else.
- **Deep reading.**
  Slow, engaged, with notes. For material you must truly understand. Speed here comes from focus, not tricks.
- **Reducing subvocalization.**
  Saying every word mentally caps speed at talking pace. Practice chunking phrases; use a pacer (finger/pen) to pull eyes forward.
- **Reducing regression.**
  Re-reading sentences compulsively. Trust forward motion; mark unclear spots and revisit once at the end if needed.
- **Active reading.**
  Questions before, predictions during, summaries after. Engagement improves both speed and retention — passive reading is slow reading.

## Practical workflow

1. **Set the purpose.**
   For each reading task: gist, facts, or mastery? Choose the strategy before opening the document.
2. **Preview everything.**
   2-minute preview: headings, abstract, conclusion. Decide: deep read, skim, or skip. Most things get skimmed or skipped.
3. **Practice skimming.**
   Daily 10-min practice on articles: headings + first sentences + visuals, then summarize. Time yourself; track comprehension.
4. **Learn scanning patterns.**
   Practice finding specific facts fast (dates, names, numbers) in dense documents. Z-pattern for pages, F-pattern for screens.
5. **Use a pacer.**
   Finger, pen, or cursor under lines at slightly-above-comfort speed. The pacer breaks subvocalization and regression habits.
6. **Read actively.**
   Question, predict, summarize per section. One-line summary per chapter/section — the retention multiplier.
7. **Schedule deep reading.**
   Deep work blocks, distraction-free, for mastery material. Speed techniques don't apply; engagement does.
8. **Review and space.**
   Revisit summaries on spaced intervals. Speed-reading without review is just fast forgetting.

## Common pitfalls

- **Speed as the goal.**
  Chasing WPM while comprehension collapses. Speed serves purpose; comprehension is the actual product.
- **Skimming what needs depth.**
  Speed-reading a contract, textbook chapter, or complex argument. Match strategy to stakes.
- **Subvocalization shame.**
  Some subvocalization aids comprehension for hard material. Reduce it for easy material; keep it for hard.
- **No preview.**
  Diving into page one of everything. Two-minute previews save hours of misallocated deep reading.
- **Passive high-speed.**
  Eyes moving fast, brain elsewhere. Active engagement (questions, summaries) matters more than eye speed.
- **Ignoring retention.**
  Reading fast and remembering nothing. Summarize and space-review, or the speed was wasted.
- **One speed for everything.**
  Novels, contracts, textbooks, feeds — all at the same pace. Strategic readers shift gears constantly.
- **Gimmick chasing.**
  Apps flashing words at 1000wpm with 20% comprehension. Sustainable gains come from strategy + practice, not tricks.
