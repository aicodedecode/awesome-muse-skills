## Overview

Webinars are demand-generation workhorses when done well: they educate, capture intent, and create reusable content. This skill covers the full production cycle — choosing topics that attract buyers, promoting to hit registration goals, running polished live sessions, and converting attendees (and no-shows) into pipeline.

## When to use

- Planning a webinar program or one-off webinar
- Choosing webinar topics that attract the right audience
- Promoting a webinar to hit registration targets
- Running the live session (hosting, tech, engagement)
- Converting attendees into sales conversations
- Repurposing webinar content

- Producing executive-level webinars
- Managing webinar series at scale
- Troubleshooting live technical issues
- Producing multi-speaker panels
## Core concepts

**Topic selection.** Teach, don't pitch. Best topics: customer pain points (from sales/support data), "how to" frameworks, industry trends with data, customer success stories. Title test: would your ideal buyer register even if they knew it was hosted by a vendor?

**Registration math.** Typical conversion: 30–40% of registrants attend live. To get 200 attendees, need 500–650 registrants. Promotion starts 3–4 weeks out; 50%+ of registrations arrive in the final week.

**The 45/15 format.** 40–45 minutes of content, 15 minutes of live Q&A. Attention drops sharply after 60 minutes. One core idea, well-explored, beats five rushed ones.

**Engagement mechanics.** Polls (2–3 per webinar: opener, mid-point, closer), live Q&A, chat monitoring, downloadable resources. Engagement data (questions asked, polls answered) is lead-scoring gold.

**The soft pitch.** Earn the right to 5 minutes of product at the end by delivering 40 minutes of genuine value. Position the product as the natural next step, not an interruption. Always include a clear CTA (demo, trial, consultation).

**Repurposing.** Every webinar becomes: on-demand gated asset, 3–5 short clips, blog post summary, quote graphics, email nurture content, sales enablement asset. Plan repurposing before the live date.


**Speaker coaching.** Brief on: energy (camera presence), pacing (slower than feels natural), interaction (address chat by name), and brevity (stories over slides).
Great speakers are made in rehearsal, not born — invest prep time proportionally to audience size.
Dry runs are non-negotiable for executives.
**Technical redundancy.** Backup internet, backup audio, backup slides, co-host failover, and platform status monitoring.
Live failures are inevitable; visible failures are optional — redundancy is professionalism.
Rehearse the failure modes, not just the happy path.
**Accessibility.** Live captions, accessible slides (contrast, font size), keyboard-navigable platforms, and post-event transcripts.
Accessibility expands audience and meets obligations — build it in, not on.
## Practical workflow

1. **Plan.** Topic (validated against audience pain), title (benefit-led), speakers (subject expert + customer beats two vendors), date/time (Tue–Thu, mid-morning or early afternoon in audience timezone), goal (registrations, attendees, SQLs).
2. **Build assets.** Registration page (title, 3 bullet takeaways, speaker bios, short form), confirmation + reminder emails (1 week, 1 day, 1 hour), slide deck (visual, minimal text — 1 idea per slide), polls, CTA offer.
3. **Promote.** Email to house list (2–3 sends), partner co-promotion, paid social to target titles, SDR invites to target accounts, speaker promotion. Track registrations by source daily.
4. **Rehearse.** Full dry run: tech check (audio, screen share, polls), timing (cut ruthlessly to fit), handoffs between speakers, Q&A prep (seed 3–4 questions), backup plan (slides advance if a speaker drops).
5. **Go live.** Dedicated producer (tech/chat) separate from host. Open with the promise and agenda. Run polls, monitor chat for questions, keep energy high, end with CTA + Q&A. Record everything.
6. **Follow up fast.** Within 24h: attendees get recording + CTA + relevant resources; no-shows get recording + next webinar invite. Score engagement (attended full, asked questions, clicked CTA) and route hot leads to sales immediately.
7. **Repurpose and measure.** Publish on-demand version, cut clips, write the recap post. Measure: registration→attendance rate, engagement, SQLs, pipeline influenced, cost per SQL.

**Dry-run checklist:** audio quality, screen share, poll functionality, slide timing, speaker handoffs, Q&A flow, recording started, backup slides ready, producer monitoring chat.


**Webinar timeline (4 weeks out):** W-4: topic, title, speaker confirmed, landing page live. W-3: promotion begins (email, social, partners). W-2: dry run #1 (content), registration push. W-1: dry run #2 (tech), reminder emails. Day-of: 30-min pre-show check, live engagement (polls every 10–15 min), Q&A moderation. Day+1: recording + slides to registrants, sales follow-up on hot attendees. Day+7: on-demand promotion.

**Engagement mechanics that work:** polls every 10–15 minutes, live Q&A with upvoting, chat prompts from a moderator, downloadable resources mid-webinar, and a clear CTA in the final 5 minutes. Webinars with 3+ engagement touchpoints retain 2x the audience to the end.

**Day-of timeline:** T-60: producer online, systems check → T-30: speakers join, AV check → T-15: final briefing, slides loaded → T-5: go live standby → live: producer manages tech, moderator manages audience → post: immediate debrief notes.
Assign producer, moderator, and speaker as separate roles — multitasking fails live.
**Post-production:** edit recording (top/tail, fix glitches) → chapters → transcript → clips for social → on-demand page → analytics review.
Post-production multiplies webinar ROI — budget time for it.
## Common pitfalls

- **Pitch disguised as education.** Attendees feel baited and never return. Deliver real value; pitch briefly at the end.
- **Death by PowerPoint.** Text-heavy slides read aloud. Visual slides, spoken insight.
- **No rehearsal.** Tech failures and timing overruns on live webinars destroy credibility. Always dry-run.
- **Ignoring no-shows.** They're often the majority and still interested. Nurture them with the recording.
- **Slow follow-up.** Waiting a week to contact hot attendees. Strike within 24 hours.
- **Single use.** Running a webinar and archiving it. Repurpose aggressively — the content cost is already sunk.
- **Wrong success metric.** Celebrating registrations while attendance and pipeline lag. Optimize for attendees and SQLs.
- **60-minute slide lectures.** Attention collapses after 20 minutes of monologue. Structure as conversation: interview format, demos, or panels beat solo slide reads.
- **No on-demand strategy.** Live attendance is 30–40% of registrations; the recording drives the rest of the value. Gate it, promote it, and repurpose clips.
- **Skipping rehearsals.** "We'll figure it out live" — famous last words. Every live element gets rehearsed; every speaker gets briefed.
- **Producer as afterthought.** Assigning production to whoever is free. Professional production needs dedicated producers — the audience can tell.
- **Panelist imbalance.** One voice dominating. Active moderation balances airtime — brief panelists on format.
- **No contingency for dropouts.** Speaker cancellations without backups. Always have a backup speaker or segment ready.
