# Zettelkasten

## Overview

The Zettelkasten ('slip box') is a note-taking method for thinking: atomic notes, densely linked, that surprise you with connections.

Developed by sociologist Niklas Luhmann, who credited it for his extraordinary output — the box became a conversation partner.

Principles: one idea per note, every note linkable, links that explain the relationship, and writing in your own words.

Tools don't matter much — index cards work. What matters is atomicity, linking discipline, and regular revisiting.

## When to use

- Academic research, thesis, or dissertation work
- Writing books, long essays, or substantial articles
- Building deep expertise in a field over years
- Any knowledge work where ideas need to connect and compound
- Moving beyond highlighting to genuine thinking in notes

## Core concepts

- **Atomicity.**
  One idea per note. If a note contains two ideas, split it. Atomic notes link cleanly; compound notes link messily.
- **Unique addresses.**
  Every note gets a unique ID (timestamp, sequence). Links point to IDs, so notes can move and rename without breaking.
- **Own words.**
  Rewrite everything in your language. Copy-paste is collecting; restating is thinking. Luhmann's rule: the box contains your thoughts, not quotes.
- **Link with context.**
  Don't just link — say why: 'contradicts', 'extends', 'example of', 'prerequisite for'. The relationship is the knowledge.
- **Fleeting -> literature -> permanent.**
  Fleeting notes (raw capture) -> literature notes (source summaries) -> permanent notes (your ideas, atomic, linked). Only permanent notes live in the box.
- **Branching, not filing.**
  New notes attach to existing ones via links, forming branches of thought. No fixed hierarchy — structure emerges from connections.
- **The box as partner.**
  Revisit randomly and by following links. Serendipitous encounters with old notes generate new ideas — if notes are atomic and well-linked.
- **Write from the box.**
  Outlines assemble from linked note sequences. Writing becomes arranging existing thoughts, not generating from blank pages.

## Practical workflow

1. **Set up the box.**
   Any tool with unique IDs and backlinks (Obsidian, plain files, even cards). One inbox for fleeting notes, one home for permanent notes.
2. **Capture fleeting notes.**
   Read with pen: jot raw reactions, quotes with page numbers, questions. Messy is fine — these are temporary.
3. **Write literature notes.**
   After reading: summarize the source's argument in your words, one note per idea-cluster, with full reference.
4. **Create permanent notes.**
   Convert: one idea per note, your words, unique ID, descriptive title. Link to 2-3 related notes with the relationship stated.
5. **Link deliberately.**
   Each new note: which existing notes does it contradict, extend, exemplify, or depend on? Write the link sentence explicitly.
6. **Follow the branches.**
   Weekly: pick a note, follow its links for 20 minutes. Add new links and notes as connections occur to you.
7. **Harvest for writing.**
   When writing: gather the branch, arrange note sequences into an outline, draft from the notes. Cite the box, not memory.
8. **Maintain the garden.**
   Monthly: merge duplicates, split compound notes, fix dead links, strengthen weak link-descriptions. The box improves with tending.

## Common pitfalls

- **Collecting without atomicity.**
  Long multi-idea notes that can't link precisely. Split ruthlessly — atomicity is the whole game.
- **Link-stuffing.**
  Ten unexplained links per note. Links without stated relationships are noise, not knowledge.
- **Skipping your own words.**
  Boxes full of quotes and highlights. If it's not in your words, it's not your thought.
- **No literature notes.**
  Permanent notes without source grounding become unsourced opinions. Keep the chain: fleeting -> literature -> permanent.
- **Filing instead of linking.**
  Elaborate folder hierarchies with few links. The Zettelkasten's power is the graph, not the folders.
- **Never revisiting.**
  Writing notes and never returning. The serendipity engine needs regular visits — schedule them.
- **Perfectionist IDs.**
  Agonizing over numbering schemes. Timestamps work fine; the ID just needs uniqueness, not meaning.
- **Tool-first adoption.**
  Spending months configuring software before writing ten notes. Start with any tool; the method is the skill.
