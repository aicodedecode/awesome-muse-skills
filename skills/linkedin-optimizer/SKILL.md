## Overview

LinkedIn is a search engine wearing a social network's clothes. Recruiters
search by title, skills,
and location — then decide in seconds from your headline and first two
experience lines. This skill
optimizes profiles for discoverability (SEO for recruiters), credibility (proof,
not claims), and
conversion (profile views that turn into conversations).

## When to use

- Rewriting a headline, About section, or experience entries

- Preparing for an active job search or a stealth one (open-to-work settings)

- Pivoting careers and repositioning past experience

- Building inbound visibility as a founder, freelancer, or consultant

- Auditing a profile that gets views but no recruiter messages

## Core concepts

- - - **Headline formula.** [Target title] | [2-3 keyword skills] | [proof or
  differentiator]. "Senior
  Backend Engineer | Go, Kubernetes | Scaled payments to 40M txns/day" beats
"Passionate engineer
  seeking opportunities." Never leave the default job-title-only headline.
- - - **Recruiter search mechanics.** Recruiters filter by current/past titles,
  skills, location, and
  years. Your profile must contain the exact terms they'd type — including
synonyms ("ML" and
  "machine learning").
- - - **About section as a pitch, not a bio.** First 3 lines are the preview —
  lead with who you help
  and how. Then: specialties, 2-3 headline results, and a clear call to action
(open to roles?
  consulting? just networking?).
- - - **Experience = resume, amplified.** Same achievement bullets as the
  resume, plus media (links,
  decks, demos) and richer context. Recruiters read the current role first —
make it the strongest
  section.
- - - **Skills + endorsements + recommendations.** Pin the top 3 skills matching
  your target.
  Recommendations from managers carry outsized weight — request them right after
a win, with a
  suggested draft to make it easy.
- - - **Activity signals.** A dormant profile looks abandoned. Light, consistent
  engagement
  (commenting thoughtfully, sharing one insight a month) keeps you visible in
feeds and searches.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. 1. **Define the target.** Who should find this profile? (recruiters for X
   roles, clients,
   co-founders.) Every choice follows from the audience.
2. 2. 2. **Rewrite the headline.** Apply the formula with the user's real
   keywords and one proof point.
   Offer 2-3 variants to choose from.
3. 3. 3. **Draft the About section.** Hook (who you help + how), proof (2-3
   metrics), specialties list,
   CTA. First person, conversational, no third-person "John is a results-driven
professional."
4. 4. 4. **Rebuild experience entries.** Strongest current role first; 3-5
   achievement bullets per
   recent role; add media links where they exist. Align titles with
industry-standard terms
   recruiters search.
5. 5. 5. **Curate skills.** Keep 10-15, ordered by target relevance. Remove
   legacy skills that attract
   the wrong searches (that 2012 PHP skill still brings PHP recruiter spam).
6. 6. 6. **Set search settings.** Open-to-work (recruiters only for stealth),
   creator mode
   considerations, and a profile URL slug (linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname).
7. 7. 7. **Photo and banner check.** Professional headshot (face, good light,
   plain background), banner
   that signals your domain. No logos-as-photos, no vacation shots.

## Common pitfalls

- - - **Buzzword soup.** "Passionate, innovative thought leader" says nothing.
  Every adjective should
  be replaceable with a fact.
- - - **Leaving the default headline.** The auto-generated "Title at Company"
  wastes the
  highest-visibility line on the profile.
- - - **Treating it as a resume dump.** LinkedIn allows narrative, media, and
  personality — a wall of
  pasted resume text misses the medium's strengths.
- - - **Wrong keywords, wrong inbound.** List skills you want to be hired for,
  not every skill you've
  ever touched. Your profile is a magnet; point it deliberately.
- - - **No CTA.** Views without a next step are vanity. Say what you want: "DMs
  open for staff backend
  roles" or "booking Q3 consulting."
- - - **Recommendation drought.** Zero recommendations on a 10-year career reads
  as "nobody would
  vouch." Even two strong ones change the picture.
