## Overview

Screen recordings power tutorials, demos, bug reports, and courses — but most are unwatchable: tiny
text, rambling narration, 20-minute runtimes for 3-minute content. Professional screen capture is a
production discipline: prepare the environment, script the narration, record cleanly, and edit
tightly. This skill covers the full workflow.

## When to use

- Recording software tutorials or course lessons

- Capturing product demos or sales walkthroughs

- Creating bug reproduction videos for developers

- Recording presentations or webinars

- Producing onboarding or training videos

## Core concepts

- - **Prepare the stage.** Close notifications (Do Not Disturb), hide personal bookmarks/tabs, clean
  the desktop, use a fresh browser profile, zoom UI to 125-150% so text reads on video. The viewer
  sees everything — curate it.
- - **Resolution discipline.** Record at 1920×1080 (or the delivery resolution) — not a 4K screen
  downscaled weirdly, not a tiny window. If the UI is dense, zoom the app rather than recording a
  postage stamp.
- - **Script, don't ramble.** Write the narration (or detailed bullet beats) before recording.
  Rambling "ums" and dead air while you think are the #1 tutorial killer. Scripted 5 minutes beats
  improvised 20.
- - **Separate audio from video.** Record narration on a decent mic as its own track (or record
  voiceover after the screen capture). Clean audio over screen content matters more than
  pixel-perfect capture.
- - **Mouse as a presenter.** Move deliberately, pause on clicks, use cursor highlighting/zoom for
  key actions. A frantic cursor is visual noise — slow down, narrate the click before you make it.
- - **Edit tightly.** Cut mistakes, dead air, and loading waits (jump-cut or speed up). Add
  zooms/callouts on critical UI, captions for key terms. A 10-minute raw recording should become 5-6
  minutes.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. **Plan the video.** One topic per video, defined outcome ("by the end, the viewer can X"),
   outline of steps. Decide: voiceover-first (record audio, then capture to match) or capture-first.
2. 2. **Set up the environment.** Do Not Disturb on, clean desktop/browser, test accounts with
   realistic (non-personal) data, app zoomed appropriately, recording area framed.
3. 3. **Test the stack.** 30-second test recording: check resolution, frame rate (30fps fine for UI;
   60 for smooth motion), audio levels, and that system audio (if needed) captures. Watch the test
   fully.
4. 4. **Record in segments.** One section per take — mistakes only cost the segment, not the whole
   video. Clap or pause 3 seconds between segments for easy edit points.
5. 5. **Narrate well.** Speak 10% slower than conversation, smile (it shows in the voice), describe
   what you're doing as you do it, and pause after key points. Record in a quiet room; closets work
   surprisingly well.
6. 6. **Edit the capture.** Assemble segments, cut errors and waits, add zooms on small UI,
   callouts/arrows for emphasis, intro/outro bumpers if it's a series. Normalize audio, remove
   background noise.
7. 7. **Export and package.** Per platform spec (YouTube 1080p, etc.), descriptive title/thumbnail,
   chapters for longer videos, and captions (auto + corrected). Include links/resources mentioned in
   the description.

## Common pitfalls

- - **Recording the messy real desktop.** Personal emails, notification popups, embarrassing tabs.
  The audience notices everything — stage the environment.
- - **Tiny unreadable UI.** Recording a 4K screen at native scaling: text becomes ants on YouTube.
  Zoom the OS/app UI before recording.
- - **Unscripted rambling.** "So, uh, let's just... hmm, where was I..." Script the beats at
  minimum; word-for-word for key explanations.
- - **Noisy audio.** Laptop mic in an echoey room with keyboard clatter. A $50 USB mic in a quiet
  room beats a $500 mic in a noisy one — but any external mic beats built-in.
- - **Real-time waiting.** Watching a progress bar for 90 seconds in real time. Edit it out or
  speed-ramp — respect the viewer's time.
- - **One giant video.** A 45-minute "complete guide" nobody finishes. Split into focused 5-10
  minute chapters — better retention, better searchability, easier updates.
