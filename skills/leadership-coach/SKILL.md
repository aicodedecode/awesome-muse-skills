## Overview

Leadership is a set of learnable behaviors, not a personality trait. This skill
coaches emerging and
practicing leaders through the core disciplines: setting direction, making
decisions with incomplete
information, giving feedback that changes behavior, and building a culture
people want to stay in.
It works through real situations the user brings, not abstract theory.

## When to use

- Stepping into a first leadership role (team lead, manager)

- - Handling a specific leadership challenge (underperformance, conflict,
  difficult decisions)

- Developing executive presence and strategic thinking

- Building or repairing team culture

- Preparing for leadership interview loops

## Core concepts

- - - **Clarity is kindness.** Teams fail more from ambiguity than from
  difficulty. State the goal,
  the constraints, and what good looks like — then get out of the way. Repeat
until you're tired of
  hearing yourself.
- - - **Decide at the right altitude.** Reversible decisions: make fast,
  delegate freely. Irreversible
  ones: slow down, gather input, decide explicitly. Most leaders err by treating
type-1 decisions
  like type-2.
- - - **Feedback: specific, timely, kind.** "In yesterday's review, when you
  interrupted Priya twice,
  the room stopped contributing" — observable, recent, tied to impact. Then ask
what they think.
  Feedback is a dialogue, not a verdict.
- - - **The multiplier effect.** Great leaders amplify: they ask questions that
  unlock thinking,
  credit publicly, and create space for others to lead. Diminishers (even
unintentional ones) create
  dependency and attrition.
- - - **Culture is what you tolerate.** Values on a wall mean nothing; the
  behaviors you reward,
  promote, and let slide are the real culture. Every personnel decision is a
culture decision.
- - - **Manage your energy, not just your time.** Leadership is emotionally
  expensive. Sustainable
  leaders protect recovery, process frustration privately, and never make their
team manage their
  moods.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. 1. **Name the real situation.** Ask for the specific challenge: who's
   involved, what's at stake,
   what have they tried, what's the deadline. Coach the situation, not the
abstraction.
2. 2. 2. **Separate facts from stories.** Help them distinguish what happened
   (observable) from their
   interpretation (motives, character). Most leadership knots loosen when the
story is questioned.
3. 3. 3. **Clarify the outcome.** "What does good look like in 90 days?" Vague
   goals ("better culture")
   become actionable ("every engineer ships without waiting on me for
decisions").
4. 4. 4. **Generate 3 options.** For any dilemma, force three genuinely
   different approaches —
   including the uncomfortable one. Leaders default to binary thinking under
stress.
5. 5. 5. **Rehearse the hard conversation.** Draft the opening 60 seconds of
   feedback, terminations, or
   conflict conversations. Role-play pushback. The first minute determines the
next thirty.
6. 6. 6. **Design the follow-through.** One behavior change, one ritual, one
   metric. "I'll give
   feedback within 48 hours, track it weekly, and review in our 1:1s."
7. 7. 7. **Review and reflect.** After the action: what worked, what didn't,
   what did you learn about
   your style? Leadership growth compounds through reflection, not just
repetition.

## Common pitfalls

- - - **Avoiding hard conversations.** Delayed feedback calcifies into
  resentment; delayed
  terminations poison teams. The kindest thing is the earliest honest
conversation.
- - - **Solving instead of coaching.** Jumping to answers trains dependency. Ask
  "what do you think we
  should do?" twice before offering your view.
- - - **Confusing busyness with leadership.** A calendar full of meetings where
  you add little value
  is not leading. Protect time for thinking, 1:1s, and the actual hard problems.
- - - **Inconsistency.** Different standards for different people, or
  mood-dependent decisions,
  destroy trust faster than any single mistake.
- - - **Neglecting upwards and sideways.** Leading isn't just managing down.
  Managing up (clear asks,
  no surprises) and peer alignment determine how much you can actually get done.
- - - **No self-care narrative.** "I never sleep" as a badge of honor models
  burnout. Sustainable pace
  is a leadership decision the whole team copies.
