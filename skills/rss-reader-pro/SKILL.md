# RSS Reader Pro

## Overview

RSS puts you in charge of your information diet: you choose the sources, you control the timing, no algorithm decides what you see.

The reader is the inbox for the web: subscribe to blogs, news, journals, releases, even YouTube channels and podcasts.

Pro skill: ruthless feed curation, fast triage habits, and filters that turn firehoses into signal.

## When to use

- Replacing algorithmic news/social feeds with chosen sources
- Following many blogs, journals, or release notes efficiently
- Research requiring monitoring of many sources
- Reducing doomscrolling while staying informed
- Teams sharing curated industry intelligence

## Core concepts

- **Feed curation.**
  Subscribe deliberately: each feed must earn its place with consistent quality. 50 great feeds beat 500 mediocre ones.
- **Folders by purpose.**
  Group feeds: must-read, skim, reference, fun. Different folders get different triage standards and schedules.
- **Triage, don't read.**
  Scan titles, open the 10% worth reading, mark-all-read without guilt. The unread count is not a debt.
- **Filters and rules.**
  Keyword filters: highlight must-read topics, mute noise topics. Tame high-volume feeds (release notes, aggregators) with rules.
- **Read-later integration.**
  Worth-reading items go to a read-later app in one keystroke. The reader is triage; deep reading happens elsewhere.
- **Full-text extraction.**
  Use readability/full-text modes for truncated feeds. Don't click through to ad-infested pages when the reader extracts cleanly.
- **OPML portability.**
  Your subscription list as OPML: portable across readers, backed up, shareable. Never rebuild 200 feeds from memory.
- **Quiet by design.**
  No breaking-news alerts from the reader. Check on your schedule (1-2x daily), not the news cycle's.

## Practical workflow

1. **Choose a reader.**
   Cross-platform with good keyboard shortcuts, filters, and read-later integration. Commit to one.
2. **Subscribe deliberately.**
   Start with 20-30 known-great sources. Add new feeds only after sampling for two weeks.
3. **Organize into folders.**
   Must-read / skim / reference / fun. Assign triage rules per folder (read fully vs. scan titles).
4. **Set check schedule.**
   1-2x daily triage sessions, 15-20 min. Outside sessions, the reader is closed — no news grazing.
5. **Configure filters.**
   Keyword highlights for your topics; mute filters for noise. Especially for high-volume feeds.
6. **Connect read-later.**
   One-keystroke send to your read-later app. Triage fast; read deeply later, deliberately.
7. **Prune monthly.**
   Feeds unread for a month get cut. 'Maybe later' feeds get cut. The feed list is a garden, not an archive.
8. **Export OPML.**
   Backup your subscriptions. Share curated OPMLs with colleagues; import trusted ones to bootstrap new topics.

## Common pitfalls

- **Feed hoarding.**
  500 feeds, 10,000 unread. Unread count becomes anxiety instead of signal. Prune to what you actually read.
- **Unread-count guilt.**
  Treating unreads as obligation. Mark-all-read is a feature, not a failure — the good stuff resurfaces.
- **No triage discipline.**
  Opening every item 'just in case.' Scan titles ruthlessly; open <10%.
- **Algorithm envy.**
  Keeping algorithmic feeds 'for discovery' alongside RSS. Pick your model; the hybrid usually means the algorithm wins.
- **Truncated-feed clicking.**
  Clicking through to cluttered sites when full-text extraction exists. Read in the reader.
- **Never pruning.**
  Feeds die, quality drops, interests change. Monthly pruning keeps the signal high.
- **News grazing.**
  Checking the reader 20x daily like social media. Scheduled sessions; the news waits.
- **No read-later handoff.**
  Trying to read long pieces in the triage flow. Send to read-later; keep triage fast and shallow.
