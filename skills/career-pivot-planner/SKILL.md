## Overview

Career pivots feel like leaps but work best as bridges. Whether you're moving from engineering to
product, finance to data science, or any field to any other, the winning approach is: inventory
transferable assets, identify the real gaps (usually smaller than feared), and sequence the move
through bridge roles and proof-of-skill projects. This skill builds that plan.

## When to use

- Considering a move to a different function or industry

- Planning a return to work after a career break

- Moving from IC to management or vice versa

- Shifting from corporate to startup (or the reverse)

- Evaluating whether a pivot is worth the cost

## Core concepts

- - **Transferable assets first.** Domain knowledge, stakeholder skills, analytical rigor,
  leadership experience — most pivots reuse 60-80% of what you already have. Inventory before you
  despair.
- - **The gap is usually 2-3 things.** Talk to 5 people doing the target role and ask what actually
  matters. The real requirements are almost always narrower than job postings suggest.
- - **Bridge roles beat cold jumps.** An adjacent role that uses your current strengths while
  building new ones (e.g., solutions engineer → product manager) de-risks the transition for both
  you and employers.
- - **Proof over credentials.** A portfolio project, freelance engagement, or internal transfer
  beats another degree for most pivots. Employers hire demonstrated ability; credentials are a
  proxy.
- - **Narrative is the product.** "I spent 6 years in ops, where I kept hitting data problems nobody
  owned — so I learned analytics and now I solve them full-time" turns a scattered history into a
  coherent story.
- - **Price the pivot honestly.** Expect a possible step back in seniority or pay for 12-24 months.
  Model the finances; decide if the long-term trajectory justifies it. Some pivots aren't worth it,
  and that's useful to know early.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. **Define the target precisely.** Not "something in tech" — a specific role at a specific
   company type. Vague targets produce vague plans.
2. 2. **Inventory assets.** List everything transferable: skills, domain knowledge, relationships,
   credentials, proof of work. Be generous — career changers systematically undervalue themselves.
3. 3. **Research the real requirements.** 5 informational interviews with people in the target role.
   Ask: what does great look like, what did you have to learn, what would you look for in a career
   changer?
4. 4. **Map the gap.** Compare assets to requirements. Identify the 2-3 genuine gaps and the fastest
   credible way to close each (project, course with portfolio output, freelance work, certification
   that employers actually respect).
5. 5. **Choose the path.** Rank options by risk/reward: internal transfer, bridge role,
   freelance-to-full-time, bootcamp-style intensive, direct application with strong proof. Pick the
   one with the best evidence per unit of risk.
6. 6.  **Build proof (60-90 days).** 1-2 substantial projects demonstrating target-role skills, in
   public where possible. This is the pivot's engine — everything else is packaging.
7. 7. **Craft the narrative and materials.** Resume rewritten around the target (lead with proof,
   frame past as prologue), LinkedIn repositioned, and a 30-second pivot story that makes the move
   sound inevitable.
8. 8. **Execute with a timeline.** Applications, networking, and follow-ups on a weekly cadence. Set
   a decision checkpoint at 90 days: what's working, what needs adjusting?

## Common pitfalls

- - **The eternal preparation trap.** Course after course with no proof of work. Learning without
  shipping is procrastination in a costume.
- - **Leading with the old identity.** A resume that screams "accountant" won't get data interviews.
  Reframe everything toward the target from line one.
- - **Ignoring the pay cut math.** Discovering the comp drop after accepting is a crisis; modeling
  it beforehand is a decision.
- - **Pivoting away from pain, not toward pull.** Running from a bad boss into an unresearched field
  repeats the cycle. Ensure genuine interest via informational interviews and trial projects first.
- - **Discounting the network reset.** Your current network hires for your old role. Deliberately
  build 10-15 connections in the new field — they're your bridge to opportunities.
- - **No financial runway.** Quitting to "figure it out" without 6+ months of buffer turns
  exploration into panic. De-risk the timeline before the leap.
