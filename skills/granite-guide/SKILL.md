## Overview

Granite (IBM) is an enterprise-oriented open model family spanning code,
language, and specialized business models (including time-series and
vision variants) — built with IBM's enterprise focus: training-data
transparency, indemnification offerings, and watsonx platform integration.
The positioning is "open models safe for enterprise" — Apache 2.0 licensing
on key releases plus IBM's enterprise assurances.

For builders in IBM-centric enterprises, Granite is the natural open-model
choice: watsonx integration, enterprise support, and licensing clarity. For
others, Granite deserves evaluation as a solid open family — particularly
where licensing simplicity (Apache 2.0) and enterprise assurances matter.

The distinctive note: IBM's indemnification and transparency commitments
address enterprise legal concerns directly. Evaluate whether that assurance
has value for your organization's risk posture.

## When to use

- IBM/watsonx-centric enterprises building on open models.
- Applications needing Apache 2.0 licensed open models.
- Enterprise deployments valuing vendor indemnification.
- Code and language tasks on open models with enterprise support.
- Regulated industries needing documented, assured AI components.
- Time-series and specialized enterprise tasks (Granite's specialized
  variants).

## Core concepts

- **Enterprise open models**: open weights with enterprise assurances —
  the combination is the differentiator.
- **Apache 2.0 licensing**: permissive licensing on key releases. Verify per
  release; the simplicity vs. community licenses is real.
- **Indemnification**: IBM's IP indemnification offerings for enterprise
  deployments. Understand the scope — what it covers and what it doesn't.
- **Code and language families**: Granite code models and language models
  for respective tasks. Benchmark each on your tasks.
- **Specialized variants**: time-series (Chronos-style), vision, and other
  enterprise-task models. Match variant to task.
- **watsonx integration**: native platform integration for IBM-stack teams —
  deployment, governance, and tooling.
- **Training transparency**: documented training approaches supporting
  enterprise risk assessment.
- **Enterprise support**: vendor support for open models — unusual and
  valuable for risk-averse organizations.

## Practical workflow

1. **Clarify assurance requirements.** What does your organization need:
   licensing simplicity, indemnification, support, transparency? Get specific.
2. **Benchmark model quality.** Granite code and language models vs.
   alternatives (Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek) on your tasks. Assurances don't
   replace quality evaluation.
3. **Verify licensing per release.** Confirm Apache 2.0 (or other) terms for
   your chosen models and use case.
4. **Understand indemnification scope.** Read the actual terms — coverage,
   exclusions, and claim processes.
5. **Evaluate watsonx fit.** If on IBM stack: test the integrated deployment
   and governance paths. If not: evaluate Granite as standalone open models.
6. **Test specialized variants.** For time-series or other specialized tasks:
   benchmark the Granite specialist against alternatives.
7. **Pin, monitor, document.** Version pins; quality monitoring; the assurance
   documentation filed for compliance.

Checklist for Granite in production:
- Assurance requirements specified and matched to IBM's offerings.
- Quality benchmarked against open-model alternatives.
- License verified per release.
- Indemnification scope understood.
- Specialized variants tested where relevant.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assurance as quality proxy.** Enterprise assurances address legal risk,
  not model quality. Benchmark anyway.
- **Indemnification over-read.** Assuming broader coverage than the terms
  provide. Read the scope.
- **No peer benchmarks.** Choosing on enterprise comfort without testing
  against Llama/Qwen/DeepSeek on your tasks.
- **watsonx assumed.** Committing to the platform without evaluating whether
  the integration benefits your team.
- **License per-release unchecked.** Assuming all Granite releases share
  terms.
- **Specialized variants ignored.** Missing the time-series and other
  specialists that may fit enterprise tasks well.
- **Support expectations vague.** Not clarifying what enterprise support
  actually covers before depending on it.
- **Static enterprise choice.** Deciding once on assurance grounds and never
  re-benchmarking quality as the open field advances.
