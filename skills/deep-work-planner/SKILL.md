# Deep Work Planner

## Overview

Deep work = cognitively demanding tasks done in distraction-free concentration. It's where valuable output comes from — and it's incompatible with a fragmented calendar.

Shallow work (email, admin, meetings) expands to fill all available time unless deep work is scheduled first.

Planning deep work means: choosing the right tasks, blocking the time, designing the environment, and defending the blocks.

## When to use

- Important projects stall while email and meetings consume days
- Wanting a sustainable routine of focused creative work
- Knowledge workers feeling busy but unproductive
- Designing team norms that protect maker time
- Students or researchers needing long thesis/writing blocks

## Core concepts

- **Deep vs. shallow.**
  Deep: cognitively demanding, creates new value, hard to replicate. Shallow: logistical, easy to replicate, necessary but not valuable. Audit your week by this split.
- **Time blocking.**
  Deep work gets calendar blocks — 90+ minutes, ideally 2-4 hours. Unscheduled deep work doesn't happen; shallow work is the default.
- **Task selection.**
  Not all hard tasks deserve depth. Choose: high-leverage, deadline-meaningful, requiring sustained thought. One deep task per block.
- **The shutdown ritual.**
  End-of-day complete shutdown: review, capture open loops, close everything. Protects evenings and makes mornings start clean.
- **Attention residue.**
  Switching tasks leaves mental residue that degrades the next task. Single-task blocks + real breaks beat 'multitasking.'
- **Depth metrics.**
  Track deep hours per week and per project. What gets measured gets protected. 10-15 deep hours/week is excellent for most roles.
- **Energy alignment.**
  Schedule deep blocks in peak energy (usually morning). Guard the golden hours; shallow work fills the troughs.
- **Boredom tolerance.**
  Deep work requires tolerating boredom without reaching for the phone. This is trainable — start with 50-min blocks and extend.

## Practical workflow

1. **Audit the week.**
   Classify last week's hours: deep vs. shallow. Most find <5 deep hours. That's the baseline to beat.
2. **Choose deep tasks.**
   List this week's 2-3 highest-leverage cognitively-demanding tasks. These earn the blocks; everything else is shallow.
3. **Block 3-5 sessions.**
   90-180 min blocks, mornings preferred, on the calendar as unmovable commitments. Start with 3/week; build up.
4. **Design the environment.**
   Phone out of room, notifications off, door closed / status set, materials ready. Friction against distraction, not willpower.
5. **Define block protocol.**
   One task per block, timer optional, scratch pad for intrusions, break after. Same ritual every time builds the habit.
6. **Run the shutdown ritual.**
   End of day: 10 min to close loops, plan tomorrow's first block, shut down completely. No 'quick checks' after.
7. **Track depth weekly.**
   Log deep hours per block. Review: total, per project, interruptions. Trend matters more than any single week.
8. **Defend and expand.**
   Treat blocks like client meetings. Quarterly: can you add one more block? Can meetings move off golden hours?

## Common pitfalls

- **Scheduling but not defending.**
  Blocks that every 'urgent' request overrides. An undefended block teaches everyone it's fake. Defend or delete.
- **Too-ambitious start.**
  Five 3-hour blocks in week one. Start with 2-3 ninety-minute blocks; build the muscle before the marathon.
- **Wrong tasks in blocks.**
  Deep blocks spent on email triage. Blocks are for the 2-3 chosen deep tasks only — ruthlessly.
- **Phone in the room.**
  'On silent, face down' still tempts and still interrupts (vibration, screen glow). Out of the room, or it doesn't count.
- **Skipping the shutdown.**
  Evenings bleed into work, mornings start groggy. The shutdown ritual is what makes deep work sustainable.
- **Measuring only hours.**
  4 'deep' hours of distracted half-work. Honest focus matters more than clock time — pair with interruption logging.
- **All depth, no shallow time.**
  Deep blocks with no scheduled shallow time means shallow tasks invade the blocks. Schedule admin/email windows too.
- **Ignoring energy.**
  Deep blocks at 4pm for a morning person. Align with chronotype or fight biology every session.
