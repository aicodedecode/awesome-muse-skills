# Note Taker

## Overview

Most notes are written and never read — expensive theater. Good note-taking is thinking on paper, not transcription.

Three jobs: capture (don't lose it), think (understand it), retrieve (find it later).

Different situations need different methods: meetings need decisions and actions, lectures need structure, reading needs your own words.

The value isn't in the notes — it's in the processing. A messy note you rewrite beats a pretty note never revisited.

After capture, a short processing step converts raw notes into durable knowledge and extracted actions.

## When to use

- Preparing for meetings, classes, interviews, user research, or conferences
- Taking notes while reading books, papers, or long articles
- Building a reusable personal knowledge base from notes
- Teaching others effective note-taking or setting team norms

## Core concepts

- **Selective capture.**
  You can't write everything and think simultaneously. Capture decisions, actions, numbers, quotes, questions, disagreements — plus your reactions.
- **Cornell method.**
  Cues left, notes right, summary bottom. Review by covering notes and recalling from cues — active recall built in.
- **Outline method.**
  Hierarchical bullets for structured content. Fast, scannable, maps well to digital outliners.
- **Mind mapping.**
  Spatial, non-linear capture for brainstorming. Structure emerges visually; great for connecting disparate ideas.
- **Fleeting -> permanent notes.**
  Capture messy and fast; within 24 hours rewrite keepers in your own words. The rewrite is where learning happens.
- **Meeting note anatomy.**
  Date, attendees, purpose, key points, decisions, action items (owner + date each), open questions.
- **Progressive summarization.**
  Bold key passages -> highlight the best of those -> summarize the highlights -> distill to one line. Each pass compresses understanding.
- **Your words or it didn't happen.**
  Restating in your own words is minimum viable learning. Highlighting without restating is the illusion of learning.

## Practical workflow

1. **Prepare with a purpose line.**
   Write the goal at the top: 'Decide X', 'Understand Y'. A note with a purpose stays focused; a blank page invites transcription.
2. **Choose the method.**
   Structured content -> outline. Discussion -> Cornell. Reading -> margin notes + chapter summary. Brainstorm -> mind map.
3. **Capture live, mark actions.**
   Short phrases, abbreviations, your words. Mark actions distinctly (`[ ] Name — task — date`), questions with `?`.
4. **Process within 24 hours.**
   Fix shorthand while memory is fresh, extract actions to your task system, bold key points, write a 3-line summary on top.
5. **Connect each note.**
   Link to related notes, projects, people — one link minimum. Isolated notes are forgotten notes.
6. **File or archive.**
   Move to the right place. Archive ruthlessly; 200 unprocessed notes is a graveyard, not a system.
7. **Review on schedule.**
   Weekly: scan recent notes for missed actions. Monthly: re-read summaries — spaced repetition for your own knowledge.
8. **Share meeting notes fast.**
   Decisions and owners highlighted, shared within 24 hours. Write for the skimmer: decisions first, details after.

## Common pitfalls

- **Transcription mode.**
  Capturing every word means understanding none. Notes should be sparser than the source.
- **Never processing.**
  Raw notes decay fast; unprocessed notes older than a week are nearly worthless. Schedule processing time.
- **No action extraction.**
  Meeting notes without owners and dates are fiction. Extract actions before closing the note, every time.
- **Copy-paste 'notes'.**
  Highlighting without restating teaches nothing. Add at least one sentence in your own words per source.
- **One giant document.**
  A 50-page running doc is unsearchable. Split by meeting/topic; title each clearly with a date.
- **Pretty over useful.**
  Formatting during the meeting instead of listening. Ugly notes processed later beat beautiful notes never reviewed.
- **No retrieval plan.**
  Notes in five apps with no naming convention can't be found. One home, consistent titles, search you trust.
- **Notes nobody reads.**
  Unshared meeting notes may as well not exist. If it isn't shared within 24h with decisions up top, it didn't happen.
