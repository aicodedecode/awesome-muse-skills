## Overview

A logo is a flag, not a biography — it needs to be recognizable at 16 pixels and
on a billboard, in
one color and full color, today and in ten years. This skill walks through
professional logo design:
researching the landscape, exploring concepts broadly, refining systematically,
and delivering a
versatile identity mark with proper files and guidelines.

## When to use

- Designing a logo for a new brand, product, or company

- Redesigning an outdated or ineffective logo

- Creating logo variations (icon, wordmark, monochrome)

- Evaluating logo concepts against professional criteria

- Preparing logo delivery files and usage guidelines

## Core concepts

- - - **Simplicity = memorability.** The best logos reduce to one idea. If you
  can't sketch it from
  memory after seeing it twice, it's too complex.
- - - **The five tests.** Every logo must pass: distinctive (not confusable with
  competitors),
  versatile (works tiny, huge, 1-color, reversed), timeless (not chained to a
trend), appropriate
  (right tone for the audience), and simple (reproducible from memory).
- - - **Concept before computer.** The idea matters more than execution. Explore
  30-50 rough concepts
  on paper before opening vector software — the best mark is usually concept
#27, not #3.
- - - **Design in black first.** If it doesn't work in solid black, color won't
  save it. Color is
  applied after the form is proven.
- - - **Negative space is free design.** The cleverest marks use the space
  between shapes (think
  arrows, hidden letters). But cleverness must never cost clarity — if people
don't see it in 3
  seconds, it's decoration.
- - - **Wordmark vs symbol vs combination.** Wordmarks suit distinctive names;
  symbols suit broad
  applications (app icons, favicons); combination marks give flexibility. Most
brands need a
  responsive set, not a single lockup.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. 1. **Discovery.** Brand values, audience, competitors' marks (to avoid),
   where the logo will live
   (app icon? embroidery? signage?), and any must-haves or must-avoids. Write a
one-sentence
   creative brief.
2. 2. 2. **Research the landscape.** Collect 15-20 logos in the category. Map
   the clichés (every
   fintech uses a geometric "forward" mark) — then deliberately avoid them.
3. 3. 3. **Sketch broadly.** 30-50 thumbnails across categories: wordmarks,
   letterforms, abstract
   symbols, literal symbols, combinations. Quantity first; judgment later.
4. 4. 4. **Select 3-5 directions.** Pick the strongest distinct concepts.
   Develop each digitally in
   black only — refined enough to judge, not polished enough to fall in love
with.
5. 5. 5. **Test ruthlessly.** The five tests above, plus: the favicon test (16px
   legible?), the
   embroidery test (works as 1-color thread?), the competitor lineup (distinct
at a glance?), the
   memory sketch test.
6. 6. 6. **Refine the winner.** Optical adjustments (curves, spacing, alignment
   — trust your eye over
   the math), then color exploration, then lockups (horizontal, stacked,
icon-only, monochrome,
   reversed).
7. 7. 7. **Deliver professionally.** Vector masters (SVG/EPS/PDF), raster
   exports (PNG at multiple
   sizes, including favicon), clearspace and minimum-size rules, color specs,
and a one-page usage
   guide (don'ts included).

## Common pitfalls

- - - **Designing the first idea.** The obvious concept is obvious to everyone,
  including competitors.
  Push past the first 10 ideas to find the distinctive one.
- - - **Trend-chasing.** Gradient blobs, thin-line geometric animals, brutalist
  chaos — trends date a
  logo fast. Aim for the 10-year test.
- - - **Too literal.** A camera shutter for a photography brand, a house for
  real estate — literal
  marks are forgettable. Find the second-order idea.
- - - **Ignoring small sizes.** A detailed mark that becomes mush as an app icon
  or favicon fails its
  most common modern use. Design the small version deliberately.
- - - **Color-dependent design.** If the logo needs its gradient to be
  recognizable, the form is weak.
  Prove it in black first.
- - - **No usage guidelines.** Delivering files without clearspace, minimum
  size, and don'ts
  guarantees the logo gets stretched, recolored, and abused within a year.
