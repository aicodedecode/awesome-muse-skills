## Overview

This skill covers the full cycle of SEO content production: choosing topics from keyword research, writing search intent-matched briefs, drafting articles that satisfy both readers and search engines, and optimizing on-page elements. The philosophy is simple: rank by being the best answer, not by gaming signals.

It assumes keyword research has been done (or will be done as part of the brief). The skill focuses on turning keywords into content that earns rankings and conversions.

## When to use

- Writing a new blog post or guide targeting a specific keyword
- Creating a content brief for a writer or freelancer
- Refreshing an underperforming page to reclaim rankings
- Planning a content cluster around a pillar topic
- Optimizing landing pages for organic search traffic

- Writing comparison or best-X pages that balance SEO with honest evaluation
- Briefing subject-matter experts so drafts need minimal SEO rework
- Auditing why a well-written page is not ranking (intent mismatch vs. authority gap)
- Building topic clusters for authority
- Writing for featured snippet capture
- Creating content for competitive SERPs
## Core concepts

**Search intent.** Every keyword carries an intent: informational (learn), navigational (find), commercial (compare), or transactional (buy). The page must match the intent — a 3,000-word guide won't rank for "buy running shoes". Check the current top 10 results: their format tells you what Google believes the intent is.

**Topic clusters.** A pillar page covers a broad topic; cluster pages cover subtopics and link back to the pillar. This builds topical authority. Plan clusters before writing individual posts.

**E-E-A-T signals.** Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Demonstrate first-hand experience (original data, photos, case studies), named authors with credentials, and clear sourcing. This matters most in health, finance, and legal content.

**On-page elements.** Title tag (keyword near the front, ~50–60 chars), meta description (compelling summary, ~150 chars), H1 (one per page), descriptive H2/H3 hierarchy, keyword in the first 100 words, internal links with descriptive anchors, optimized images (alt text, compression), and a clear URL slug.

**Content depth vs. fluff.** Cover the topic completely without padding. Use the top-ranking pages as a floor for coverage, then add what they miss: original examples, data, templates, or a unique angle.


**Information gain.** Search engines increasingly reward content that adds something new versus the existing ranking set: original data, unique examples, first-hand testing, expert quotes. Before writing, ask: what will this page say that the top 5 do not? If the answer is nothing, do not publish yet.

**SERP feature targeting.** Featured snippets favor 40–60 word direct answers under a question H2; list snippets favor clean numbered or bulleted steps; video carousels favor embedded video with chapters. Format sections deliberately for the features present on your target SERP.

**Search intent matching.** Informational (learn), navigational (find), commercial (compare), transactional (buy).
Match format to intent: guides for informational, comparisons for commercial, product pages for transactional.
Intent mismatch is the #1 reason good content does not rank.
**E-E-A-T signals.** Experience (first-hand use), expertise (credentials), authoritativeness (citations, links), trustworthiness (accurate, transparent).
Show, do not claim: author bios, original photos, data citations, update dates.
YMYL topics (health, finance) face higher bars — meet them or do not compete.
**Content depth.** Cover topics comprehensively: answer the query, anticipate follow-ups, address objections, provide examples.
Depth beats length — 1,500 focused words outperform 3,000 padded ones.
Update top performers quarterly; freshness signals matter.
## Practical workflow

1. **Confirm the keyword and intent.** Search the keyword, scan the top 10. Note the dominant format (listicle, guide, tool, comparison), average depth, and SERP features (featured snippets, videos, People Also Ask).
2. **Write the brief.** Include: target keyword + secondary keywords, intent classification, target word count (based on top 10 average, not a fixed number), outline with H2s mapped to subtopics competitors cover, unique angle or gap to exploit, internal links to add, CTA for the page.
3. **Draft for readers first.** Open with the answer or the promise — no throat-clearing. Use short paragraphs, descriptive subheads, bullet lists for scannable points, and concrete examples over abstractions. Write at the reader's level, not the expert's.
4. **Optimize on-page.** Place the primary keyword in the title tag, H1, first paragraph, at least one H2, and naturally throughout. Add secondary keywords where they fit. Write the meta description as ad copy for the click.
5. **Add E-E-A-T and UX.** Author byline with bio, publish/updated dates, citations for claims, original images or screenshots, a table of contents for long posts, and FAQ schema where a real FAQ section exists.
6. **Internal linking.** Link to the pillar page and 2–4 relevant cluster pages with descriptive anchor text. Link out to 1–2 authoritative sources where it helps the reader.
7. **Publish and monitor.** Track rankings weekly for 8–12 weeks. If a page stalls on page 2, refresh it: expand thin sections, add the missing subtopics from higher-ranking pages, improve the title tag's click appeal.

**Brief template essentials:** keyword, intent, audience, angle, outline (H2s), word count target, internal links, CTA, competing URLs to beat and how.


**Snippet-optimization pass:** after drafting, add a 50-word direct answer under each question-style H2, convert eligible processes to numbered steps, and add a comparison table where the SERP shows one. This pass alone often wins featured snippets within weeks.

**Content brief template:** target keyword + intent → SERP analysis (what ranks and why) → outline (H2s matching intent) → unique angle (what we add) → SME requirements → internal linking plan → CTA → success metrics.
Briefs align writers, SEOs, and SMEs before words are written — preventing expensive rewrites.
**Publishing checklist:** keyword in title/H1/URL → meta description (compelling, < 155 chars) → headers structured → images optimized (alt text, compressed) → internal links (3–5) → schema markup → mobile rendering checked.
Checklists catch the small things that compound into rankings.
## Common pitfalls

- **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating the exact phrase unnaturally. Use variations and synonyms; modern search understands semantics.
- **Ignoring SERP intent.** Writing a guide when the SERP shows product pages (or vice versa) is a guaranteed page-2 result.
- **Thin rewrites of competitors.** If your article says nothing the top 5 don't, there's no reason to rank it. Add original value.
- **Forgetting the click.** Rankings without clicks are worthless. Title tags and meta descriptions are ad copy — test them.
- **Publishing and abandoning.** Content decays. Schedule refreshes for high-value pages every 6–12 months.
- **No internal links.** Orphan pages rank poorly. Every new page should be linked from at least one relevant existing page.
- **Chasing volume over relevance.** A 500-search keyword that matches your product beats a 10,000-search keyword that doesn't convert.
- **Targeting keywords with no commercial value.** Ranking #1 for a term that never converts. Map keywords to funnel stages before investing.
- **Ignoring page experience.** Slow, janky pages leak the rankings great content earns. Core Web Vitals are a tiebreaker that decides close races.
- **Writing for algorithms.** Keyword-stuffed, robotic content. Write for humans; optimize for algorithms — in that order.
- **Neglecting updates.** Publishing and forgetting. Topical content decays; schedule refreshes for winners.
