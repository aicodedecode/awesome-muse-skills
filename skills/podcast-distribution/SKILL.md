## Overview

Great episodes mean nothing if nobody finds them. Podcast distribution covers the technical and promotional plumbing: RSS feeds, directory submissions, show-notes SEO, audiogram clips, and cross-promotion — the systems that put episodes in front of listeners. (For podcast marketing strategy, see podcast-marketer.)


Podcast distribution gets episodes to every listening app: RSS feeds, directories (Apple, Spotify, YouTube), and the optimization that makes shows discoverable.
Beyond plumbing, distribution strategy covers platform-specific optimization, video podcasts, and the analytics that guide growth.

## Core concepts

**RSS feeds.** The backbone: a valid RSS feed with proper enclosure tags, episode metadata (titles, descriptions, durations), artwork specs, and category tags. Validate the feed before launch — broken feeds break every directory listing. Your hosting platform usually generates this; verify it.

**Directory landscape.** Apple Podcasts and Spotify dominate, but distribute everywhere: Google Podcasts/YouTube Music, Amazon Music, Stitcher, Overcast, Pocket Casts, plus regional players. Submit once per directory; use your host's auto-distribution where available. Claim profiles on Spotify for Podcasters and Apple Podcasts Connect for analytics.

**Show notes SEO.** Episode titles (keyword + hook), descriptions (keyword in first 2 lines, timestamps, links), and dedicated web pages per episode. Google indexes show notes — they're a discovery channel most podcasters waste.

**Clips and audiograms.** 30–90 second vertical clips with captions and waveforms for social. Extract 3–5 per episode: the hook, the best insight, the funniest moment. Clips are the top discovery driver for new shows — design episodes with quotable moments.

**Cross-promotion.** Promo swaps with similar-sized shows (30-second trailers), guest appearances (their audience becomes yours), feed drops (full episode swaps), and network joining (when scale justifies it). Promote shows your target listeners already hear.

**Release strategy.** Consistent day/time (trains audience habits), batch-scheduling in your host, launch with 3+ episodes (binge-ability), and seasonal vs. continuous (seasons allow breaks without guilt; continuous builds habit).


**RSS fundamentals.** Your RSS feed is the source of truth: episode titles, descriptions, artwork, and audio files.
Hosting platforms generate the feed; directories poll it.
Validate feeds (podba.se, castfeedvalidator) — broken feeds silently stop updating everywhere.
Own your feed URL — platform lock-in via feeds is a real risk.
**Directory optimization.** Apple Podcasts (categories, ratings, reviews matter), Spotify (playlists, algorithmic recommendations), YouTube (SEO, thumbnails, chapters).
Each platform has distinct discovery mechanics — optimize per platform, not generically.
Submit everywhere listeners are: Apple, Spotify, YouTube, Overcast, Pocket Casts, Amazon.
**Video podcasts.** YouTube is now a top podcast discovery channel.
Record video (even simple static setup), optimize titles/thumbnails for YouTube SEO, add chapters, and link audio versions.
Video doubles production effort — start audio-only, add video when the show proves itself.
**Analytics.** Downloads (directional, not precise), unique listeners, consumption rate (do people finish?), follower growth, and cross-platform comparison.
Podcast metrics are notoriously fuzzy — track trends, not absolutes.
## Practical workflow

1. **Set up hosting.** Choose a host with: reliable RSS, auto-distribution, analytics (downloads, consumption, sources), and embed players. Configure artwork, categories, and descriptions for discoverability.
2. **Validate and submit.** Validate RSS, submit to all major directories, claim analytics profiles, and verify listings render correctly (artwork, descriptions, episode order).
3. **Build the episode template.** Title formula, description template (hook → summary → timestamps → links → CTA), show notes web page, and clip extraction plan. Consistency speeds production.
4. **Produce clips.** Per episode: 3–5 audiograms, 1 trailer-style cut, quote graphics. Post natively per platform (vertical for Reels/Shorts/TikTok, square for feeds). Always caption.
5. **Run cross-promotion.** Identify 20 similar shows, pitch promo swaps, book guest appearances, and track referral sources. One good guest spot can outperform months of organic growth.
6. **Measure.** Downloads per episode (7/30/90-day windows), consumption rate, follower growth, directory chart positions, website visits from show notes, and listener surveys (how did you find us?).

**Launch checklist:** RSS validated → directories submitted → 3 episodes ready → audiograms cut → guest promo scheduled → show notes pages live → analytics claimed → social accounts ready.


**Launch distribution checklist:** hosting configured → RSS validated → submitted to all directories (allow 1–2 weeks for approval) → website episode pages with show notes → YouTube versions → social clip pipeline → newsletter integration.
Directories approve at different speeds — submit early, launch when all are live.
**Ongoing optimization:** monthly analytics review → title/description A/B tests → back-catalog SEO (old episodes compound) → guest cross-promotion → playlist pitching.
Podcast growth is slow then sudden — consistency for 12+ months before judging.
## Common pitfalls

- **Broken RSS.** Invalid feeds silently break listings. Validate before and after any host changes.
- **Directory neglect.** Only on Spotify. Distribute everywhere — listeners have entrenched app habits.
- **Weak titles/descriptions.** "Episode 47" tells nobody anything. Keyword + hook in every title.
- **No clips.** Publishing audio-only and hoping for discovery. Clips are the marketing engine.
- **Inconsistent schedule.** Random drops train nobody. Pick a cadence and keep it.
- **Ignoring analytics.** Not knowing which episodes retain or where listeners come from. Check consumption rates monthly.
- **No web presence.** Episodes only in apps. Show-notes pages capture search traffic and give sharing a home.
- **Platform exclusivity.** Exclusive deals limit reach for unproven shows. Distribute widely until you have leverage to negotiate exclusivity profitably.
- **Neglecting show notes.** Thin descriptions hurt SEO and sharing. Write real show notes: summaries, timestamps, links — they compound in search.
- **Inconsistent audio quality.** Variable levels and background noise. Listeners forgive content flaws; they do not forgive audio flaws — invest in decent mics and editing.
