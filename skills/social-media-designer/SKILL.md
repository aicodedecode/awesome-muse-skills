## Overview

Social design is gladiatorial: your work fights hundreds of other posts for a
split-second decision
to stop or scroll past. Winning requires platform-native thinking — formats,
specs, and behaviors
differ wildly between Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, and X — plus thumb-stopping
hooks and brand
consistency at speed. This skill covers the craft and the production system.

## When to use

- Designing posts, carousels, stories, or reels covers

- Creating templates for consistent brand content

- Adapting designs across platforms and formats

- Improving engagement on visual content

- Planning a content series or campaign visuals

## Core concepts

- - - **The 1-second hook.** The first frame (or first 3 words on it) decides
  everything. Lead with
  the payoff, the provocation, or the pattern-break — never with a logo or a
throat-clearing title.
- - - **Platform-native formats.** Instagram: 1080×1350 (4:5) carousels,
  1080×1920 stories/reels;
  LinkedIn: 1200×627 or square documents; X: 1600×900. Design in the native
aspect ratio — cropped
  reposts look amateur.
- - - **Carousels are mini-narratives.** Slide 1: hook. Slides 2-n: one idea
  each, building. Last
  slide: CTA (save, share, follow, comment). Design for the swipe — each slide
should create
  curiosity for the next.
- - - **Text on images: less, bigger.** Social graphics get 1-2 seconds.
  Headline-sized text (readable
  in a feed thumbnail), minimal supporting words. If it needs paragraphs, it's a
caption or a
  carousel, not a single image.
- - - **Brand consistency at speed.** Templates with locked brand elements
  (colors, fonts, logo
  placement) and flexible content zones let you produce daily without
redesigning daily. Consistency
  builds recognition; recognition builds trust.
- - - **Accessibility on social.** Alt text on every image, captions on every
  video, sufficient
  contrast, no text in tiny sizes. Also: don't put key text where UI overlays it
(bottom 20% of
  stories/reels, edges of profile grids).

## Practical workflow

1. 1. 1. **Know the platform and goal.** Awareness? Saves? Clicks? Profile
   visits? The goal determines
   the format and CTA — and the platform determines the specs.
2. 2. 2. **Write the hook first.** Before designing: what's the scroll-stopping
   first frame? Draft 3
   options, pick the strongest. Design serves the hook, not vice versa.
3. 3. 3. **Design in native specs.** Correct dimensions, safe zones (avoid UI
   overlay areas), RGB,
   readable at feed size. Test by viewing at actual phone size — not zoomed on a
27" monitor.
4. 4. 4. **Build the template system.** Master templates per format with brand
   locks and content zones.
   Variants for series (quote cards, tips, announcements) that feel related but
not identical.
5. 5. 5. **Batch production.** Design a week's content in one session:
   consistent lighting on effort,
   consistent quality. Batching beats daily scrambling.
6. 6. 6. **Write the caption as part of the design.** Visual and caption work
   together — the graphic
   hooks, the caption delivers depth and the CTA. Hashtags: relevant and few,
not 30 generic ones.
7. 7. 7. **Review analytics, iterate.** Track saves, shares, and profile taps
   (not just likes). Double
   down on formats that convert; kill what doesn't. Refresh templates quarterly
before fatigue sets
   in.

## Common pitfalls

- - - **Designing for desktop.** Reviewing social graphics on a big monitor at
  100% zoom. Always check
  at phone size — that's the only size that matters.
- - - **Logo-first frames.** Opening with branding instead of value. Earn the
  attention first; the
  brand rides along.
- - - **Text walls on images.** Paragraphs crammed into a square. Respect the
  medium: visual first,
  details in caption or carousel.
- - - **Ignoring safe zones.** Key text hidden under the profile pic, like
  buttons, or story UI. Know
  each platform's overlay map.
- - - **Inconsistent branding.** Every post a different style because there's no
  template system.
  Followers should recognize your content before reading the handle.
- - - **Chasing trends blindly.** Jumping on every meme format regardless of
  brand fit. Trends are
  vehicles — only ride the ones going your direction.
