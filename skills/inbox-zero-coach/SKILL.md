# Inbox Zero Coach

## Overview

Inbox zero isn't a permanent state — it's a daily practice of making decisions about every email instead of letting them pile up into a second to-do list. As your coach, Muse walks you through triage sessions: you share what's in the inbox, and together you decide for each message — delete it, delegate it, respond now, or defer it to a specific time. Over weeks, this builds three durable habits: batching email instead of living in it, templating the replies you write over and over, and unsubscribing ruthlessly from everything that doesn't earn its place. The goal is a quiet inbox and a mind that doesn't orbit around unread counts.

## When to use

- Your inbox has more than 50 unread messages and the number is climbing
- You check email constantly but rarely finish anything because of it
- You want a structured daily triage routine instead of ad-hoc skimming
- The same kinds of emails keep eating your time (newsletters, notifications, FYI threads)
- You're about to run a triage session and want a coach to keep you honest and fast

## Core concepts

- **The 4D triage.**
  Every email gets one decision: Delete (trash or archive), Delegate (forward to the right person with a clear ask), Respond (if it takes under two minutes, do it now), or Defer (schedule a specific time to handle it, don't just leave it). Never "leave it for later" without a time.
- **Batching.**
  Email is checked at set times — two or three windows a day — and closed in between. Constant availability to your inbox is constant interruption.
- **The two-minute rule.**
  If replying takes less than two minutes, reply during triage instead of filing it away. Filing a quick reply for later costs more than just doing it.
- **Templates for repeat replies.**
  Most of your sent mail is variations on a handful of messages. Templates turn five minutes of drafting into thirty seconds.
- **Unsubscribe as a sport.**
  Every newsletter you delete without reading is a recurring cost. Unsubscribe the moment you notice you never read something. Your future self pays the subscription price in attention.
- **The daily 15-minute routine.**
  Same time, same place, every day: triage what's new, nothing more. Fifteen focused minutes beats an hour of guilty skimming spread across the day.
- **Archive, don't organize mid-triage.**
  During a session, the only destination for "not now" mail is the archive (with a deferral time if needed). Filing into the perfect folder mid-session is procrastination in disguise.

## Practical workflow

1. **Open a triage session.**
   Tell Muse: "Let's do inbox triage. I have about 30 unread." Give it the context it needs: your role, what matters this week, and which senders are always important.
2. **Go through messages in batches.**
   Share the senders and subject lines (or paste a few at a time) and ask: "For each one: delete, delegate, respond, or defer?" Muse suggests a decision per message; you confirm or override. The rhythm matters more than perfect decisions.
3. **Handle the two-minute replies immediately.**
   For the "respond" pile, ask Muse: "Draft a short reply to this one." You review, send, and archive. Done during the session, never postponed.
4. **Delegate with clear asks.**
   For handoffs, prompt: "Draft a forwarding note for my colleague covering this — what I need from them and by when." Vague forwarding just bounces the problem.
5. **Defer with a real time attached.**
   "I need to defer these five — suggest when to handle each based on my calendar this week." A deferral without a time is just procrastination with paperwork.
6. **Build your template library.**
   After a session, ask: "Which of my replies today are templates I should keep?" Muse extracts the reusable patterns. Save them somewhere you can reach in seconds.
7. **End with an unsubscribe sweep.**
   Ask: "Which senders in today's inbox did I never actually read? Draft unsubscribe reasons." Then do it — one click each. Future triage sessions get shorter every week.

## Common pitfalls

- **Triage without decisions.**
  Reading everything and moving nothing is just organized anxiety. Every message leaves the session with a decision attached — no exceptions.
- **Deferring without a time.**
  "Later" is where emails go to die. If you can't name when you'll handle it, you haven't decided anything.
- **Responding to newsletters.**
  Some emails exist only to be deleted or unsubscribed. Don't let a triage session turn into reading time.
- **Checking between batches.**
  One triage session doesn't help if you reopen the inbox every ten minutes. Pick two or three check times and hold the line.
- **Keeping emails as a to-do list.**
  Emails are not tasks. Tasks go in your task system; the inbox holds only undecided messages. If it's decided, it leaves.
- **Never unsubscribing.**
  The delete key treats the symptom. Unsubscribing treats the cause. Every triage session should end at least one subscription.
- **Over-engineering folders.**
  Twenty labels is a filing hobby, not productivity. Archive, and let search do the work.
- **Triage without a timer.**
  An untimed session expands to fill the morning. Set a 15-minute timer; when it rings, you're done until the next window. Speed is a feature.
- **Replying from the phone at midnight.**
  Triage happens in your windows, on a real keyboard. Midnight phone replies are where tone goes to die and typos live forever.
- **Treating every email as equal.**
  A note from your manager and a promo from a store are not the same priority. Tell Muse who your VIP senders are so triage weights them properly.
- **Skipping the session when it's busy.**
  The busier the day, the more you need triage — that's when the inbox grows fastest. Shorten the session when busy, don't cancel it.
