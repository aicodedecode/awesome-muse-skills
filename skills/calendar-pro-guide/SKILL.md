## Overview

## Core concepts

**Time blocking.** Assign every hour a job: deep work blocks, meeting blocks, admin blocks, buffer blocks. The calendar reflects priorities — if "strategy" has no block, it's not a priority. Protect blocks like meetings (decline conflicts by default).

**Meeting hygiene.** Every meeting needs: purpose, agenda, required vs. optional attendees, and a default length (25/50 min, not 30/60 — buffers matter). Recurring meetings need owners and periodic review — most outlive their usefulness.

**Calendar audit.** Categorize two weeks of calendar: deep work, meetings (by value), admin, personal. Compare to ideal allocation. The gap reveals the problem — usually meetings crowding out everything else. Audit quarterly.

**Scheduling.** Minimize back-and-forth: share availability links, propose 2–3 specific times, respect timezones (show both zones), and batch similar meetings. For groups: poll once, decide fast, don't reopen.

**EA/executive calendaring.** Gatekeeping with judgment (what deserves the exec's time?), strategic scheduling (energy-matched: hard thinking in peak hours), buffer management, prep time blocks before important meetings, and saying no gracefully on their behalf.

**Visibility and boundaries.** Share appropriate availability, publish working hours and focus blocks, use tentative/OOO honestly, and set response expectations. A calendar that's always "free" invites overload.


**Time blocking.** Assign every hour a job: deep work blocks (2–4 hours, notifications off), meeting blocks, admin batches, and buffer time.
A calendar with only meetings is a to-do list written by other people — block time for your own priorities first.
Review weekly: does the calendar reflect your actual priorities? Adjust ruthlessly.
**Scheduling etiquette.** Share agendas in invites; default to 25/50 minutes; include video links and dial-ins; respect time zones (show them explicitly); decline or propose alternatives rather than accepting meetings you will not attend.
Every accepted-but-skipped meeting trains others that your RSVPs mean nothing.
**Calendar sharing and visibility.** Default sharing settings by role (free/busy for most, details for direct teams); private appointments for sensitive items; team calendars for PTO and out-of-office.
Transparency reduces scheduling friction; oversharing creates noise. Set thoughtful defaults.
**Scheduling tools.** Booking links (Calendly-style) for external meetings, round-robin for team distribution, and automated reminders to cut no-shows.
Rules: always offer 3+ time options across days, confirm time zones explicitly, and include preparation expectations in the booking flow.
## Practical workflow

1. **Audit.** Two-week time analysis: categorize everything. Identify: low-value recurring meetings, fragmentation (no 2-hour focus blocks), and misalignment with stated priorities.
2. **Design the ideal week.** Template: deep work blocks (2–4h, mornings if possible), meeting windows (afternoons), admin batches, buffers between meetings, personal/family blocks. One template, reused weekly.
3. **Fix meetings.** Kill or shorten low-value recurrings, convert status meetings to async updates, enforce agendas, set 25/50 defaults, and require purpose for new invites.
4. **Implement blocking.** Schedule the ideal week as recurring blocks. Treat focus blocks as unbookable (mark busy, decline conflicts with suggested alternatives).
5. **Streamline scheduling.** Availability links, scheduling tools for externals, timezone-aware proposing, and batch days (e.g., "all 1:1s on Tuesdays").
6. **Maintain.** Weekly review (plan the week Sunday/Monday), quarterly audit, and continuous pruning of recurring meetings.

**Meeting invite checklist:** clear title → purpose (one line) → agenda → required vs. optional → pre-read attached → correct duration → video link → timezone clarity for distributed attendees.


**Weekly calendar audit (15 min, Friday):** review next week → block deep work before meetings fill it → decline or delegate low-value meetings → batch similar meetings → confirm external meetings → protect one meeting-free half-day.
**Meeting hygiene checklist:** purpose stated in invite → agenda attached → right attendees (can anyone be optional?) → decision or outcome defined → notes and actions captured → follow-up scheduled if needed.
Meetings without purposes are just interruptions with invitations.
## Common pitfalls

- **No focus blocks.** Calendar full of meetings, deep work squeezed into cracks. Block focus time first.
- **Default 60 minutes.** Everything takes an hour because it's scheduled for an hour. Default 25/50.
- **Meeting without purpose.** Accepting invites with no agenda. Ask "what's the goal?" — decline if unclear.
- **Fragmentation.** 30-minute gaps between meetings that fit nothing. Batch meetings; protect long blocks.
- **Recurring meeting rot.** Standups from 2019 nobody questions. Review all recurrings quarterly.
- **No buffers.** Back-to-backs all day. 5–10 min buffers prevent cascade delays and allow reset.
- **Always available.** Never blocking personal time. Boundaries on the calendar are boundaries in life.
- **No buffers.** Back-to-back meetings all day. Schedule 5–10 minute buffers — overruns are normal, buffers make them harmless.
- **Accepting everything.** An always-yes RSVP policy. Every yes is a no to something else — choose deliberately.
- **Time-zone blindness.** Scheduling across zones without checking. Use world-clock views; rotate painful slots fairly for recurring global meetings.
- **Calendar as status symbol.** Busy calendars signaling importance. Protect white space — thinking time is productive time.
- **Phantom meetings.** Recurring meetings nobody attends. Audit recurring series quarterly — cancel or revitalize.
- **No travel time.** Back-to-back locations without transit. Block travel explicitly — lateness compounds through the day.
