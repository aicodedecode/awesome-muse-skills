## Overview

Freelancing trades one boss for many — and trades salary certainty for control and upside. Success
depends less on craft (which most freelancers have) than on business skills: positioning, pricing,
pipeline, and client management. This skill is a practical operating manual for starting and
sustaining independent work.

## When to use

- Deciding whether to go freelance and planning the transition

- Setting rates and choosing a pricing model

- Finding first clients and building pipeline

- Writing contracts, scopes, and proposals

- Handling difficult clients, scope creep, or late payments

## Core concepts

- - **Positioning beats prospecting.** "Designer for SaaS onboarding flows" gets hired; "freelance
  designer" gets ignored. A sharp niche makes marketing 10x easier and commands higher rates.
- - **Pricing models.** Hourly (simple, caps income), project/fixed (rewards efficiency, needs good
  scoping), retainer (stable, best for ongoing work), value-based (highest upside, hardest to sell).
  Most successful freelancers migrate toward fixed/retainer over time.
- - **The rate formula.** Target annual income ÷ billable hours (not 2080 — use 1000-1200 after
  admin, marketing, vacation) × 1.3 (taxes, benefits, dry spells). Then sanity-check against market
  rates for your niche.
- - **Pipeline is survival.** The feast-famine cycle kills freelancers who stop marketing when busy.
  Rule: always have 2-3 warm prospects, even at full capacity. Marketing is a weekly habit, not a
  panic response.
- - **Contracts before work.** Scope, deliverables, timeline, payment terms, revision rounds, kill
  fee, IP transfer on final payment. A one-page agreement prevents 90% of disputes. Never start
  without a signature and (ideally) a deposit.
- - **Cash flow discipline.** Separate business account, quarterly tax set-asides (25-35% depending
  on jurisdiction), 3-6 month buffer. Late payments are normal — your buffer is what makes them
  survivable.

## Practical workflow

1. 1. **Validate the leap.** Before quitting: land 1-2 paying clients or save 6 months of expenses.
   Define your niche and ideal client profile in one paragraph each.
2. 2. **Set up the basics.** Business registration/tax status for your jurisdiction, separate bank
   account, simple invoicing tool, one-page contract template, portfolio site.
3. 3. **Price it.** Run the rate formula, research 5-10 competitors in your niche, and pick a
   starting rate. Plan to raise it every 3-6 months as demand proves out.
4. 4. **Get the first 3 clients.** Warm network first (announce the launch properly — most
   freelancers under-announce), then: targeted outreach, freelance platforms as a supplement (not
   the strategy), partnerships with agencies, and content in your niche.
5. 5. **Run the engagement.** Kickoff call → written proposal with scope and price → 30-50% deposit
   → weekly updates → delivery → final invoice (net-15 or net-30 terms, late fee stated).
   Over-communicate; silence breeds anxiety.
6. 6. **Manage scope creep.** "Happy to do that — it's outside our current scope, so here's what it
   would add in time and cost." Friendly, immediate, in writing. Creep handled late becomes
   resentment.
7. 7. **Build the flywheel.** Every engagement ends with: testimonial request, referral ask, case
   study permission, and a check-in scheduled for 90 days out. Past clients are the cheapest new
   business.

## Common pitfalls

- - **Underpricing to "get started."** Low rates attract price-sensitive clients who are the hardest
  to serve and never refer premium work. Price for the clients you want.
- - **No contract, no deposit.** "They seemed trustworthy" precedes most freelancer horror stories.
  Process protects both sides.
- - **Saying yes to everything.** Bad-fit clients cost more than they pay. Red flags: haggling hard
  on price, vague scope, "exposure" offers, urgency without respect.
- - **Stopping marketing when busy.** The pipeline gap hits 4-8 weeks later, exactly when the big
  project ends. Non-negotiable weekly marketing time.
- - **Blurring work/life boundaries.** No set hours becomes all hours. Define working hours, a
  shutdown ritual, and real vacations — burnout bankrupts freelancers faster than slow months.
- - **Ignoring taxes and admin.** Quarterly filings, expense tracking, retirement contributions.
  Boring, mandatory, and the thing most new freelancers learn about the painful way.
