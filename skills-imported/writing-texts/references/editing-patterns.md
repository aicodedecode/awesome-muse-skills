# Formulaic phrasing: an editing pass

A reference inside `writing-texts`. The voice rules, the genre, the author's intent and the scope of the request outweigh a mechanical word search. The examples do not tell who wrote a text.

One short emphasis, a deliberate repetition, a professional term or a real contrast needs no fix. First check whether the device gets in the way of meaning or reading. Without a request, do not edit quotes, code, identifiers or wording the author chose to keep. Do not invent measurements or sources to follow a recommendation.

## Content

1. **Inflated significance.** «Открывает новую эпоху», "marks a turning point", "plays a vital role". Name the specific change; remove general praise.
2. **Significance tacked on at the end of a sentence.** "highlighting", "underscoring", «что подчёркивает важность». Remove the empty comment; keep a real consequence or cause.
3. **Promotional adjectives.** «Инновационный», «уникальный», "seamless", "groundbreaking", "powerful yet simple". Replace with a property from the source material or drop the judgement.
4. **Vague appeal to authority.** «Эксперты считают», "studies show". Name the source you have. If there is none, ask the author or suggest removing the claim; do not make a source up.
5. **Stock forecast.** "Despite challenges, continues to thrive", a promise of a bright future at the end. Keep a confirmed conclusion, a limit or a real next step.
6. **Advertising contrast.** "Ten features. Zero headaches." Drop the pose, name the property. Do not erase a contrast that develops the author's thought.
7. **Announced depth.** «Вот чего никто не понимает», "the real question is", "at its core". Give the thought itself without announcing its importance.
8. **Commentary on the obvious.** «Это важнее, чем кажется», "as you can see", «другими словами» when the gloss adds nothing. Remove the lead-in; keep a real explanation.
9. **Arguing with an absent opponent.** "Some might say", «можно было бы подумать». Remove the invented dispute if the argument does not need it.

## Words and constructions

10. **Heavy words instead of ordinary ones.** "Delve", "tapestry", "testament", "leverage", "utilize", "facilitate", abstract "landscape", "realm", "journey". Replace with an ordinary word unless it is a precise term or an apt image of the author's.
11. **Inflated copula.** «Является», «выступает в качестве», "acts as", "serves as", "boasts", when "is" or "has" is enough. Write it directly.
12. **The "not X, but Y" formula.** "It's not just a linter, it's a system." Check whether a real contrast is needed. If X is there only for effect, keep Y. Keep a contrast the author makes on substance.
13. **Triplets for rhythm.** Three adjectives, examples or short phrases in a pattern. Keep as many items as carry separate meaning.
14. **Synonyms for one concept.** Do not rename an entity for variety. In technical text, repeating the exact term beats ambiguity.
15. **A list dressed as a range.** "From onboarding to billing" when these are two examples, not the ends of a range. Name them as examples.
16. **Verbal ballast.** "In order to" → "to", "due to the fact that" → "because"; «важно отметить» is usually not needed. Remove extra words, keep the relation between thoughts.
17. **Stacked hedges.** «Возможно, вероятно, может», "could potentially possibly". Keep the hedge that states the author's real degree of certainty.
18. **A judgement instead of a property.** «Существенно ускоряет», "runs quickly". If there is a measurement, name it. If not, do not invent a number; use the description you have or ask for the fact. Do not pass a personal judgement off as a measurement.
19. **Nominalizations.** «Осуществить выполнение проверки» → «проверить»; "perform a validation of" → "validate". Bring back the action and the actor.
20. **A technical metaphor instead of a plain action.** "Vector", "surface", "lever", "flywheel" for an ordinary method, interface or change. Name the action. Keep the author's clear figurative comparisons if they serve the thought.
21. **A feeling instead of what a feature does.** "Deploys feel effortless", «ошибки обрабатываются элегантно». In a product description, name the behavior. In a personal article, the author's feeling is the content and needs no technical replacement.

## Structure

22. **A sentence you have to reread.** Split the linked thoughts or simplify the syntax. Leave a long sentence that is clear the first time.
23. **A passive that hides the participant.** «Запросы проверяются», "requests are validated". Name who checks if they are known and matter. Do not invent an actor.
24. **The point hidden behind the justification.** Move the conclusion or decision earlier, then explain. In a targeted edit, do not restructure the whole article without a request.
25. **The first sentence repeats the heading.** Start with content if the repetition adds nothing.
26. **An ending that retells the text.** «Подводя итог», "overall", a list of what was already said. End with a conclusion or action that follows from the article; do not add a new one for a striking ending.
27. **Chopped explanation.** Short sentences can add emphasis or just read like artificially compressed notes. «Подключи плагин. Внутри три модуля. Настраивать их не нужно» reads more naturally as «Ты подключаешь плагин целиком, и три модуля внутри него начинают работать сразу, поэтому настраивать каждый отдельно не нужно». This is an example of keeping the links, not a sentence-length template. One short emphasis and short commands stay fine.
28. **A colon that announces a reveal.** "The detail that makes it work: a separate grader." Check whether the construction helps clarity. Do not ban an ordinary explanatory colon for its form alone.

## Formatting

29. **The dash as a constant rhythm prop.** In new text, use simpler punctuation. Do not mechanically touch the author's dashes in a targeted edit, or ranges, code and tables.
30. **A bold label that repeats the sentence.** "Performance: performance improved." Remove the repetition or keep the label only if it helps scanning.
31. **Too much bold.** Bold what the reader has to find quickly, not every term or name.
32. **Title Case in English headings.** Use sentence case by default, keeping names and official titles.
33. **Decorative emoji.** Do not add them to the text or headings. An explicit user format takes priority.
34. **Formatting heavier than the content.** Lists instead of two connected sentences, headings over every short paragraph, dividers between all parts. Match the formatting to the content.
35. **Mixed quotation marks.** Bring them to the convention the text uses («ёлочки» in Russian prose, "curly" in English). In technical examples use straight quotes; do not change code or literal values.

## Assistant replies

36. **Stock opening and closing.** "Certainly", "Great question", "Happy to help", «обращайся, если что». Start with useful content and stop when the task is done.
37. **Flattery instead of a response.** "You're absolutely right" before every answer. Answer on substance; explain agreement or disagreement when it matters.
38. **Announcing the next point.** «Давайте погрузимся», "without further ado". Go to the thought itself.
39. **Retelling the process in the result.** Do not list the work steps if they do not affect the reader's decision. Keep significant checks, ruled-out causes and limits.
40. **Plausible gap filling.** "While details are limited, it likely..." Do not turn missing data into an invented fact. Ask for what is needed or mark the edge of what is known.
41. **Showing diligence instead of the result.** Bare counts of checks, tallies and lists of everything done. Report the decision and the grounds that matter, not the amount of effort.
