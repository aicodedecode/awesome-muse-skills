# Articles

Read when writing or reworking an article or a guide, and when working on a title, a summary or a translation. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

## Editing for meaning

Before writing or reworking an article, work out for yourself which reader's task the text solves, which question the reader comes in with, and how the sections lead to the answer. A personal story about an experience and a practical guide to a tool can use the same material but need different emphasis. This is a short working check, not a plan to be approved. Natural language does not replace a developed thought. When asked only for language errors, do not restructure the article.

**Pick material by its role.** From a transcript, take what develops the article's question: a cause, a needed example, a limit or a consequence. An interesting story on the same broad topic may lead to another conclusion and not fit this article. Keep an experience attributed to the person who had it; removing the name does not make someone else's case the author's.

**Order by meaning.** If a long text has several stages of reasoning, give them headings. For each section it should be clear what it adds and why it stands here. When the task changes between sections, explain the change; a word like «поэтому» / "therefore" does not create the link by itself. Merge a repetition that has no new role; put a needed premise before the conclusion. Do not give every article the same number or order of sections.

**The ending completes the reasoning.** Return to the opening question and state what follows from the experience discussed: what is now clearer and what that changes in the original thought. The conclusion can be assembled from what the author already said; do not add a new fact, piece of advice or personal position for the sake of an ending. Test by deletion: if without the ending the reader loses only a recap of the sections, the ending is not doing its job yet. A short link to what came before is needed, but a list of all examples does not replace it. Neither a separate conclusions section nor a call to action is required.

If the material allows different endings, offer a specific reading as an editor's option. When the choice cannot be made without the author's position, ask one question about it. For example: "Are you leading to this approach being worth adopting, or to it fitting only a narrow case?" / «Ты ведёшь к тому, что этот подход стоит внедрить, или к тому, что он подходит только в узком случае?» Do not put a stock moral in place of a missing thought.

The author's clarifications can change the meaning, not just add detail. Revisit the related introduction, sections and ending within the allowed scope. New memories and intentions of the author could not have been guessed; a repeated edit of a thought already explained is a reason to check whether the clarification was taken in.

## A practical article about a tool

Apply this section when the article's job is to help the reader choose or use a tool. Show the main tasks and ways of working; a personal case can illustrate them. A striking result in one case must not narrow the description of what the tool does. This is not a requirement to list every feature or to always put the case after the explanation. If the story of how the tool was made is already told in another article the reader can reach, link to it and keep only the needed context here.

Check that the reader understands how the tool is built and what the first step is: what they install, which parts they get and how they use them. Describe the structure the way the tool's documentation or code does, consistently across the whole article. Do not attribute the capabilities of the whole to one part.

Tell a possibility from a required order of work. If the reader can get the whole result at once or first discuss a plan, keep both options. Check against the version of the docs or code the reader can access; keep internal details when they explain a result or a user's choice. A README gives facts, not necessarily the article's structure. A check built into the tool does not by itself prove the result is error-free.

If the author asks readers for feedback, make it clear what to send and where. Do not turn useful extras into a mandatory questionnaire. Keep the author's distinction between what is needed and what is optional.

## Title, summary and translation

Pick the title and summary by the current meaning of the whole article. After the main idea changes, bring them in line if they are part of the request. A summary briefly gives the article's question and the author's angle; it does not list the headings or promise an unproven result. Do not turn a goal the author is working toward into an effect already achieved.

Translate the latest agreed version into natural language, keeping the structure of the argument, the facts, the degree of certainty and any deliberate device in the title. A translation is not permission to bring back rejected edits or to re-edit the meaning. Check that title, summary and body agree across the languages.
